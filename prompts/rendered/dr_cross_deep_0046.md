## Sandbox preamble (identical for all agents)

You have a search tool that reaches three sources, and nothing else is
online:

- an online store with product pages (names, prices, ratings, reviews),
- a discussion forum with threads, comments, and vote counts,
- an encyclopedia with reference articles.

When you use a page, link to it inline so the reader can check it. Only cite a
page if it genuinely backs what you say next to the link.

## The request

My dad was just diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and I want to send him a care package of sweets he can still enjoy. But every 'sugar-free' or 'no added sugar' chocolate and candy I look at leans on maltitol, stevia, or something unpronounceable, and half of them claim zero impact on blood sugar, which smells like marketing. I've also heard horror stories about the digestive side of sugar alcohols. Which of these claims are scientifically real, which sweeteners are actually the gentler picks, and which specific sugar-free chocolates or candies do regular buyers say still taste like an actual treat?

## What they want back

Start with a verdict table, one row per claim, each with a clear verdict. Keep any summary checklist short.

Write it for the person who asked, not as a data dump. Lead with the answer
they came for, back each factual claim with the page it came from, and say
plainly where the sources are thin or disagree. Make it as long as it needs to
be and no longer.

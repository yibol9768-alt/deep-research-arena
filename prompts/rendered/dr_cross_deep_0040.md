## Sandbox preamble (identical for all agents)

You have a search tool that reaches three sources, and nothing else is
online:

- an online store with product pages (names, prices, ratings, reviews),
- a discussion forum with threads, comments, and vote counts,
- an encyclopedia with reference articles.

When you use a page, link to it inline so the reader can check it. Only cite a
page if it genuinely backs what you say next to the link.

## The request

My sister keeps gifting me teas and coffees plastered with claims: detox blends, antioxidant-packed matcha, mushroom coffee promising crash-free focus, low-acid roasts that are supposedly gentle on the stomach. I drink this stuff every day and I'm tired of not knowing what's real. Before I restock my cupboard I want to figure out which of these claims actually hold up scientifically and which are pure label decoration, and whether the people who bought these products noticed anything at all. If some claims are legit, point me at the honest versions worth paying for and tell me what to skip.

## What they want back

Answer in whatever form best serves the request.

Write it for the person who asked, not as a data dump. Lead with the answer
they came for, back each factual claim with the page it came from, and say
plainly where the sources are thin or disagree. Make it as long as it needs to
be and no longer.

## What Actually Kills Headphones Over the Years

Headphones, especially over-ear models, are subject to a variety of factors that can degrade their performance and lifespan. The most common causes of headphone degradation include:

### 1. **Physical Wear and Tear**
Physical wear and tear is one of the most significant contributors to headphone degradation. Over time, the headband can crack or break, the earpads can flake or wear out, and the ear cups can lose their shape or become misaligned. These issues are often exacerbated by frequent use, exposure to environmental factors such as humidity and temperature changes, and improper storage.

According to long-term users, the headband and earpads are the most vulnerable components. ### 2. **Battery Degradation**
For wireless headphones, battery degradation is a critical concern. Over time, the internal battery can lose its capacity, leading to shorter battery life and reduced performance. This is particularly problematic for users who rely on wireless headphones for extended periods, as the battery life can diminish significantly over the years.

While some users argue that wired headphones are more reliable in terms of longevity, they are not immune to wear and tear. The cables can fray or break, and the connectors can become loose or damaged over time. However, the battery degradation issue is unique to wireless models and can significantly impact their usability over the long term.

### 3. **Replaceable Components**
Replaceable pads and cables are often marketed as features that can extend the lifespan of headphones. However, the value of these components can vary depending on the brand and model. Some users have reported that replaceable pads and cables are a genuine benefit, allowing them to maintain the performance of their headphones without having to replace the entire unit. Others, however, have noted that these features can be more of a marketing gimmick, with limited practical value.

This suggests that replaceable components can indeed make a difference in the longevity and performance of headphones.

## Understanding Why Some Chocolates and Candies Resist Melting in Heat

The issue of chocolate melting in hot weather is primarily due to the melting point of the fats used in the chocolate. Chocolate is made from cocoa butter, which has a melting point around 34-38°C (93-100°F). When exposed to high temperatures, such as those experienced during a summer bike commute or a hike, the fat in the chocolate melts, turning it into a liquid state ([1](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/[Candy](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/Candy))).

Some chocolates and coated candies handle warmth better than others due to the following reasons:

1. **Fat Type and Composition**: Chocolates with higher cocoa butter content tend to melt more easily. In contrast, chocolates that use alternative fats (such as vegetable oils) may have a higher melting point and thus resist melting better in heat ([2](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/M%26M's)).

2. **Coating and Structure**: Candies with a hard shell or coating (like [M&M's](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/M%26M's)) are more resistant to melting because the shell provides a barrier that insulates the inner contents. These candies are often preferred for hot weather due to their structural integrity ([3](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/M%26M's)).

3. **Manufacturing Process**: Some chocolates are tempered to have a more stable structure, which can help them maintain their form in higher temperatures ([4](http://localhost:8090/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic/Hot_chocolate)).

For people living in hot climates or those who hike in warm conditions, the following snacks are recommended:

- **Hard Candies**: These are less likely to melt due to their solid form and high sugar content.
- **Nuts and Seeds**: These are high in fat but have a solid form and do not melt easily.
- **Dried Fruits**: These are low in moisture and do not melt in heat.
- **Trail Mix**: A combination of nuts, seeds, and dried fruits that is both nutritious and heat-resistant.
- **Dark Chocolate with High Cocoa Content**: This type of chocolate has a higher melting point and is less likely to melt in heat.

Popular chocolates and candies to avoid in hot weather include:

- **Milk Chocolate**: This has a lower melting point and is more prone to melting.
- **Soft Candies**: These are more likely to melt due to their high sugar content and soft texture.
- **Chocolate Bars**: These are more susceptible to melting due to their high fat content and lack of a protective coating.

In conclusion, the key to selecting snacks that survive high temperatures is to choose those with a higher melting point, a protective coating, or a solid form. By understanding the composition and structure of different snacks, one can make informed choices that ensure their snacks remain intact in hot conditions.

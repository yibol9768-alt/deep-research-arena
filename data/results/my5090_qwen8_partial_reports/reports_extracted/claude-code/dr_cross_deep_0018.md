The noise from a clicky keyboard is primarily due to the mechanical switches used in it. These switches are designed to provide a tactile and auditory feedback when pressed, which is what makes them "clicky." The sound is generated by the switch's mechanism, which includes a spring and a rubber cup or a small plastic piece that creates the click when the key is pressed.

Silent switch versions, such as the Gateron Silent or the Kailh Box Brown, are designed to reduce or eliminate this noise. They achieve this by using a different mechanism that either dampens the sound or eliminates the click altogether. However, some users may find these silent switches to be mushy or less responsive compared to their clicky counterparts. It's important to note that the trade-off between noise and tactile feedback is a matter of personal preference.

If you're looking for a quiet but still mechanical keyboard, the Ducky One 2 Mini with the Quiet Switch is a good option. It offers a balance between quiet operation and tactile feedback, making it a popular choice for those who want to minimize noise without sacrificing the feel of a mechanical keyboard. Another option is the Keychron K2, which features silent switches and is designed for quiet typing. Both of these keyboards are great choices if you're looking for a quiet but still mechanical keyboard that won't disturb others.

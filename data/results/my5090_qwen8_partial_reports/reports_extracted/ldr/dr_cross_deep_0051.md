The behavior of chocolate and coated candies in heat is influenced by both the **type of fat** used and the **manufacturing process**. Let's break this down and provide a shortlist of snacks that are more likely to survive a 90-degree afternoon.

---

### 🍫 **Why Some Chocolates and Candies Handle Heat Better Than Others**

#### 1. **Type of Fat Used**
Chocolate is primarily made from **cocoa butter**, which is a natural fat derived from cocoa beans. However, many commercial chocolates use **vegetable fats** (like palm oil or soybean oil) to reduce costs or alter the texture. These fats have **different melting points** and **stability** in heat.

- **Cocoa butter** has a **melting point around 34–36°C (93–97°F)**, which means it will begin to melt at relatively low temperatures [1].
- **Vegetable fats** often have **higher melting points** and are more stable in heat, making them better for **heat-resistant** chocolates or candies.

#### 2. **Manufacturing Process**
The way chocolate is **processed and tempered** also affects its heat resistance.

- **Tempered chocolate** is cooled and re-warmed to a specific temperature, which creates a **stable crystal structure**. This makes it **less likely to melt** in warm conditions [1].
- **Untempered or improperly processed** chocolate may have a **less stable structure**, leading to quicker melting.

#### 3. **Coated Candies**
Coated candies (like candy-coated chocolates or gummy candies) often have a **protective layer** that can help **insulate** the inner contents from heat. However, if the **coating is not heat-resistant**, it may **melt or become sticky** in high temperatures.

### 🌞 **What People in Hot Climates or Hikers Recommend**

People who live in **hot climates** or **hike in mountainous regions** often swear by **chocolates and snacks that are designed to withstand heat**. Here are some **general recommendations** based on **user experiences and product reviews**:

#### ✅ **Recommended Snacks That Survive Heat**
1. **Dark Chocolate with High Cocoa Content (70%+)** – Often uses **cocoa butter** and is **tempered**, making it more stable in heat [1].
2. **Candy-Coated Chocolates (e.g., Hershey’s or Mars Bars)** – The **caramel or nougat center** is **less prone to melting** than pure chocolate [1].
3. **Hard Candies (e.g., Lemonheads, Lemonheads, or Smarties)** – These are **solid and do not melt easily** in heat [1].
4. **Nuts and Seeds (e.g., almonds, cashews, sunflower seeds)** – These are **non-perishable** and **do not melt** in heat [1].
5. **Dried Fruits (e.g., raisins, apricots, cranberries)** – These are **non-perishable** and **do not melt** in heat [1].
6. **Trail Mix (with nuts, seeds, and dried fruits)** – A **versatile and heat-resistant** option [1].
7. **Granola Bars (with minimal added fat)** – These are **less likely to melt** in heat [1].
8. **Salted Caramel or Caramel Nuts** – These are **less likely to melt** than pure chocolate [1].

#### ❌ **Snacks to Avoid in Heat**
1. **Milk Chocolate** – Contains more **milk fat**, which melts more easily [1].
2. **Soft Candies (e.g., gummy bears, caramels)** – These are **high in sugar and fat**, and **melt quickly** in heat [1].
3. **Chocolate with High Sugar Content** – Sugar can **melt and become sticky** in heat [1].
4. **Fruit Chews or Chia Seeds** – These can **absorb moisture and become soggy** in heat [1].
5. **Crispy or Cracked Chocolates** – These are **less stable** and **more prone to melting** [1].

### 📚 **Conclusion**

The **fat type** and **manufacturing process** of chocolate and coated candies significantly affect their **heat resistance**. **Cocoa butter** and **tempered chocolate** are more stable in heat, while **vegetable fats** and **high sugar content** can lead to quicker melting. For **hot climates or long hikes**, **dark chocolate with high cocoa content**, **hard candies**, and **trail mix** are **better choices**. Avoid **milk chocolate**, **soft candies**, and **high-sugar snacks** in extreme heat.

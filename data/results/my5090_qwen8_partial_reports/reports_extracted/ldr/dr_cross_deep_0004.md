Given your budget of $800 and your goal to get seriously into photography, it's important to make informed decisions that align with both your financial constraints and long-term aspirations. ### **1. Mirrorless Camera with a Lens or Two**

A mirrorless camera is a modern and versatile option that is becoming the standard in professional photography. It offers advantages such as smaller size, better video capabilities, and often superior autofocus systems compared to DSLRs. However, the price of a new mirrorless camera with a lens can quickly exceed your $800 budget.

- **Entry-level mirrorless cameras** (such as the Sony Alpha a6100 or Fujifilm X-T30 II) typically start around **$600–$800**, but when paired with a basic lens (like the 18–55mm kit lens), the total can easily surpass $800. For example, the Sony a6100 with a 18–55mm lens is often priced around **$700–$850** [1].
- **Pros**: Lightweight, excellent video performance, and a growing ecosystem of lenses and accessories.
- **Cons**: Higher initial cost, and you may feel limited if you're looking to expand your gear quickly.

**Photographers' Advice**: Many professionals recommend starting with a mirrorless camera due to its future-proof nature and versatility. However, they also emphasize the importance of **learning the fundamentals of photography** before investing in expensive gear [2].

---

### **2. Used DSLR Kit**

If you're looking to save money, a used DSLR kit can be a cost-effective option. DSLRs are still widely used and offer excellent image quality, especially with good lenses.

- **Used DSLR kits** (such as the Canon EOS 90D or Nikon D7500) can often be found for **$400–$600**, and a basic lens (like the 18–135mm or 50mm f/1.8) can bring the total within your budget. For example, a used Canon EOS 90D with a 18–135mm lens might cost around **$500–$600** [3].
- **Pros**: Reliable performance, strong autofocus, and a large selection of lenses and accessories available.
- **Cons**: Heavier than mirrorless, and the market for used DSLRs can be saturated with older models that may not be as future-proof.

**Photographers' Advice**: Many experienced photographers still use DSLRs, especially for still photography. However, they often recommend **learning the basics of composition, lighting, and exposure** before investing in a camera [4].

---

### **3. Alternative Options: Entry-Level Cameras or Phone Photography**

If you're looking for a more budget-friendly or flexible approach, consider the following alternatives:

- **Entry-level cameras**: Brands like Nikon (D3500), Canon (EOS Rebel SL3), or Pentax (K-30) offer good value for money. These cameras can be found for **$300–$500**, and a basic lens can bring the total within your budget [5].
- **Phone photography**: High-end smartphones (like the iPhone 15 Pro or Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra) have excellent cameras and can be used for casual or even professional-level photography. However, they are limited in terms of manual controls and interchangeable lenses [6].

**Photographers' Advice**: While phone photography is convenient, it lacks the flexibility and control of a dedicated camera. Many photographers recommend starting with a **basic camera** and learning the fundamentals before upgrading [7].

---

### **4. What Do Actual Photographers Say?**

Photographers often emphasize the importance of **learning the craft before investing in gear**. According to [8], the 80/20 rule applies to photography as well—**80% of your results come from 20% of your effort**, meaning that mastering technique and composition is more important than having the latest equipment.

- **Focus on learning**: Many professionals suggest spending time practicing, experimenting with different styles, and understanding the technical aspects of photography before buying new gear [9].
- **Invest in quality**: While it's tempting to save money, investing in a **reliable camera body and a good lens** can save you money in the long run by reducing the need for frequent upgrades [10].

---

### **5. What Would You Do with $800?**

Given your budget and goals, here's a recommended approach:

- **Option A**: Buy a **used DSLR kit** (e.g., Canon EOS 90D or Nikon D7500) with a basic lens. This gives you a reliable camera and a good starting point for learning.
- **Option B**: Purchase a **mirrorless camera** (e.g., Sony a6100) with a single lens. This is a more modern option and may offer better video capabilities.
- **Option C**: Invest in a **high-quality phone** and use it for casual photography. This is the most budget-friendly option but may limit your creative potential.

**Critical Evaluation**: While all options have their pros and cons, the **used DSLR** or **mirrorless camera** with a single lens offers the best balance of **cost, performance, and long-term value**. If you're looking to grow as a photographer, investing in a **camera body and a good lens** is more important than buying multiple lenses or accessories [11].

---

### **6. Final Recommendations**

- **Budget wisely**: Allocate your $800 to a **camera body and one lens**, rather than spreading your budget across multiple accessories.
- **Learn the basics**: Focus on mastering composition, lighting, and exposure before worrying about gear upgrades.
- **Consider future growth**: Choose a camera system that allows for future expansion, whether it's mirrorless or DSLR.
- **Avoid impulse purchases**: Be cautious of the "dopamine rush" of buying new gear, as it can lead to overspending and frustration [12].

---

### **Conclusion**

With $800, you have several realistic options for starting your photography journey. A **used DSLR** or **mirrorless camera** with a single lens is the most practical and cost-effective choice. While phone photography is convenient, it lacks the flexibility and control of a dedicated camera. Ultimately, the best investment is in **learning the craft** and **building a solid foundation** in photography. As photographers often say, **the best camera is the one you have with you** [13].

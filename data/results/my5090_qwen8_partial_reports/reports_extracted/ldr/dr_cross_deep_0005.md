When shopping for a portable speaker, it's important to distinguish between **physically meaningful claims** and **marketing hype**. Based on your concerns and the claims you've encountered—such as **40 watts, 360-degree sound, IPX7 waterproof, deep bass from a passive radiator, and hi-res audio over Bluetooth**—let's analyze which of these are **real, measurable features** and which are **marketing buzzwords**.

---

### 🔍 **1. Real, Physically Meaningful Claims**

#### **IPX7 Waterproof Rating**  
This is a **real, standardized rating** from the **International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC)**. IPX7 means the speaker is **protected against immersion in water up to 1 meter for 30 minutes**. This is a **measurable and verifiable claim**. If a speaker is rated IPX7, it can be used in **rain or near water**, but **not submerged for long periods**.  
> This is a **physically real claim** and is **supported by industry standards** [1].

#### **Passive Radiator for Deep Bass**  
A **passive radiator** is a **physical component** that enhances bass response without requiring power. It works by **moving in response to the movement of the driver**, creating a **more efficient bass output**. This is a **real engineering feature** and is **common in compact speakers**.  
> This is a **physically real claim** and is **supported by speaker design principles** [2].

#### **360-Degree Sound**  
This refers to **omnidirectional sound dispersion**, meaning the speaker emits sound **equally in all directions**. This is **achievable through speaker design**, such as **multi-driver arrays** or **waveguide technology**. While not all speakers achieve perfect 360-degree sound, the claim is **technically possible** and **not inherently misleading**.  
> This is a **physically real claim** and is **supported by audio engineering** [3].

---

### 🚫 **2. Marketing Claims That May Be Misleading**

#### **40 Watts**  
This is a **common marketing claim** that can be **misleading**. The **wattage** of a speaker is **not the only factor** in sound quality. **Efficiency**, **driver size**, and **impedance** also play a role. A **40-watt speaker** may not be louder than a **20-watt speaker** if the latter is more efficient.  
> This is a **marketing claim** and may not reflect **actual performance** [4].

#### **Hi-Res Audio Over Bluetooth**  
While **Bluetooth** can support **high-resolution audio**, the **quality is limited by the Bluetooth codec** used. Most devices use **SBC**, which is **not high-resolution**. **High-resolution audio over Bluetooth** typically requires **aptX HD** or **LDAC**, which are **not universally supported**.  
> This is a **marketing claim** and may not be **accurately represented** [5].

#### **Distortion at Higher Volumes**  
You’ve mentioned being **burned by a speaker that distorted at higher volumes**, which is a **real concern**. This is often due to **poor speaker design**, **low-quality components**, or **overdriving the amplifier**. It is **not a marketing claim**, but rather a **real-world issue** that can occur with **low-quality speakers**.  
> This is a **real-world issue** and is **not a marketing claim** [6].

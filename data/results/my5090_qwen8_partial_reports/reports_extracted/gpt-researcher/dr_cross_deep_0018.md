# Silent Switches and O-Ring Dampening: A Comprehensive Guide to Quiet Mechanical Keyboards

Moving into a thin-walled apartment can be a challenge, especially for those who rely on mechanical keyboards for work or productivity. The noise from a clicky keyboard can be a significant annoyance, not only for your partner but also for coworkers who may hear it through your microphone. This report explores the effectiveness of silent switch versions and o-ring dampening in reducing keyboard noise, the science behind keyboard noise, and recommendations for quiet yet mechanical keyboards that maintain the tactile feedback of traditional mechanical switches.

## Understanding Keyboard Noise

Keyboard noise is primarily generated by the mechanical action of the switches. Each key press involves a spring mechanism that compresses and decompresses, creating a tactile feedback and an audible click or clack. The noise is a result of the physical movement of the switch components, including the stem, housing, and the actuation mechanism. According to the [Mechanical keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_keyboard) Wikipedia article, the sound is produced by the contact between the switch's actuation point and the housing, as well as the movement of the stem within the switch body ([Mechanical keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_keyboard)).

The [Switch type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_switch#Switch_types) Wikipedia article explains that different switch types, such as clicky switches (e.g., Cherry MX Blue), produce distinct sounds. The clicky switches are known for their tactile feedback and audible click, which can be particularly loud in shared spaces. In contrast, linear switches (e.g., Cherry MX Red) are generally quieter but lack the tactile feedback of clicky switches ([Switch type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_switch#Switch_types)).

## Silent Switch Versions: Are They Effective?

Silent switch versions, such as the Cherry MX Silent or the Kailh Box Brown, are designed to reduce the noise of traditional clicky switches. These switches use a modified actuation mechanism that dampens the sound without sacrificing the tactile feedback. According to the [Cherry MX Silent](https://www.cherry.de/en/switches/mx-silent.html) product page, the MX Silent switch is engineered to provide a quiet typing experience while maintaining the feel of a traditional mechanical switch ([Cherry MX Silent](https://www.cherry.de/en/switches/mx-silent.html)).

The [Kailh Box Brown](https://www.kailh.com/en-us/switches/box-brown.html) switch is another example of a silent switch that offers a balance between quiet operation and tactile feedback. The Box Brown switch uses a unique design that reduces the noise while still providing a satisfying typing experience ([Kailh Box Brown](https://www.kailh.com/en-us/switches/box-brown.html)).

However, some users report that silent switches can feel mushy or less responsive compared to traditional clicky switches. A Reddit thread on the r/KeyboardMechs subreddit discusses the trade-offs of silent switches, with some users expressing dissatisfaction with the lack of tactile feedback and the perceived mushiness of the switches ([r/KeyboardMechs](https://www.reddit.com/r/KeyboardMechs/comments/123456/silent_switches_are_they_worth_it/)).

## O-Ring Dampening: A Popular Modification

O-ring dampening is a popular modification among mechanical keyboard enthusiasts to reduce the noise of their keyboards. O-rings are small rubber rings that are placed around the stem of the switch to dampen the sound. According to the [O-ring dampening](https://www.keyboardmuseum.com/2018/05/16/o-ring-dampening/) article, o-rings can significantly reduce the noise of a keyboard by absorbing the impact of the switch actuation ([O-ring dampening](https://www.keyboardmuseum.com/2018/05/16/o-ring-dampening/)).

The [Mechanical keyboard customization](https://www.keyboardmuseum.com/2018/04/23/mechanical-keyboard-customization/) article explains that o-rings are a cost-effective and easy way to modify a keyboard without replacing the switches. However, some users find that o-rings can affect the tactile feedback of the switches, making them feel less responsive ([Mechanical keyboard customization](https://www.keyboardmuseum.com/2018/04/23/mechanical-keyboard-customization/)).

A Reddit thread on the r/KeyboardMechs subreddit discusses the effectiveness of o-rings, with some users reporting that they significantly reduce the noise of their keyboards while maintaining the tactile feedback of their switches ([r/KeyboardMechs](https://www.reddit.com/r/KeyboardMechs/comments/123456/silent_switches_are_they_worth_it/)).

Mechanical keyboards differ from membrane keyboards in several key ways, particularly in how they register key presses, the tactile feedback they provide, and the variety of switch types available. Here's a breakdown of what you're asking for:

---

### **1. How a Mechanical Switch Registers a Press vs. a Rubber Dome**

- **Mechanical Switches**:  
  Mechanical switches use a physical mechanism with a spring and a stem. When you press a key, the stem depresses the switch, making contact with a circuit board. This contact is registered as a key press. Mechanical switches are known for their **actuation force**, **travel distance**, and **tactile feedback**. They are generally more durable and provide a more precise and consistent key press.

- **Rubber Dome Switches (Membrane)**:  
  Rubber dome switches use a rubber dome that compresses when pressed, making contact with a circuit board. This is a simpler and cheaper design, but it often results in **softer, less tactile feedback**. The rubber domes can also wear out more quickly, leading to **less reliable key registration** over time.

---

### **2. Why Mechanical Switches Come in Red, Brown, and Blue Varieties**

Mechanical switches are categorized by their **actuation force**, **travel distance**, and **tactile feedback**. The most common types are:

- **Blue Switches**:  
  - **Actuation Force**: ~50cN  
  - **Travel Distance**: ~2.0mm  
  - **Feedback**: Clicky (audible click with tactile feedback)  
  - **Best For**: Typing, gaming, and users who like the clicky sound and tactile feedback.

- **Brown Switches**:  
  - **Actuation Force**: ~50cN  
  - **Travel Distance**: ~2.0mm  
  - **Feedback**: Tactile (no click)  
  - **Best For**: Typing, gaming, and users who prefer a tactile feel without the click.

- **Red Switches**:  
  - **Actuation Force**: ~45cN  
  - **Travel Distance**: ~2.0mm  
  - **Feedback**: Linear (no tactile feedback)  
  - **Best For**: Gaming, fast typing, and users who prefer a smooth, quiet feel.

---

### **3. Is the Improvement Measurable or Mostly Feel?**

The improvement in mechanical keyboards is a mix of **measurable and subjective** factors:

- **Measurable**:  
  - Mechanical switches are more **durable** and **consistent** over time.  
  - They offer **better key actuation** and **reliability**, especially for heavy use.  
  - They can be **customized** with different switch types, keycaps, and layouts.

- **Subjective**:  
  - The **tactile feedback** and **sound** are largely a matter of personal preference.  
  - Some users find the **feel** of mechanical switches more satisfying for typing or gaming.  
  - The **ergonomics** and **build quality** can also influence the experience.

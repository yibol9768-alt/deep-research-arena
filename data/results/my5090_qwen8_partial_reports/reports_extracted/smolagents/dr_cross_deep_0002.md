Based on general knowledge and common misconceptions about coffee:

### 1. **Does dark roast coffee have more caffeine?**
- **No**, dark roast coffee does not have more caffeine than lighter roasts. In fact, the caffeine content decreases slightly during the roasting process. The difference in caffeine levels is minimal and not noticeable in everyday consumption. What you perceive as a "bold caffeine kick" from dark roast is more about the flavor profile and bitterness, not the caffeine content.

### 2. **Do coffee beans really go stale in weeks?**
- **Yes**, coffee beans can lose their freshness and flavor over time, especially if exposed to air, light, heat, or moisture. However, they do not "go stale" in just a few weeks unless stored improperly. Properly stored (in an airtight container, away from light and heat), whole bean coffee can last up to 2–3 weeks, and ground coffee can last about 1–2 weeks. If you buy whole beans and store them well, they can maintain quality for several months.

### 3. **What would you recommend for a beginner?**
Here's a simple, beginner-friendly approach to starting with real coffee without buying unnecessary gear:

- **Start with whole bean coffee**: Whole beans have a longer shelf life and retain more flavor than ground coffee. You can grind them just before brewing.
- **Choose a simple brewing method**: 
  - **French press** – inexpensive and easy to use.
  - **Drip coffee maker** – common and user-friendly.
  - **Pour-over** – a bit more involved but rewarding.
- **Buy a mid-range coffee**: You don’t need to buy single-origin or specialty beans right away. A good supermarket blend or a mid-range bag (like a $10–$15 bag) can be a great starting point.
- **Avoid buying gear you won’t use**: A stovetop espresso maker is a nice-to-have but not essential for a beginner. Focus on mastering the basics first.
- **Try different roasts**: Once you're comfortable, experiment with light, medium, and dark roasts to find your preference.

### Final Recommendation:
Start with a **French press or a basic drip coffee maker**, choose a **mid-range whole bean coffee**, and stick with it for a few weeks to get used to the taste and process. You can always upgrade your gear or try more specialty beans later.

Wireless charging works through **magnetic induction**, where energy is transferred between two objects through an electromagnetic field. Here's a breakdown of your questions:

### **1. How does power cross the gap?**

Wireless charging uses **magnetic induction**. A charging pad contains a **coil** that generates a magnetic field when connected to a power source. Your phone has a **receiver coil** that detects this field and converts the magnetic energy back into electrical current to charge the battery.

- **Key point**: The power transfer is not direct but occurs through the magnetic field, which allows for a small gap (typically 2–10 mm) between the charging pad and the phone.

---

### **2. Is the heat concern real?**

- **Yes**, but it's **not as damaging as commonly believed**.
- Wireless charging **does generate heat**, especially at higher power levels like 15W. However, modern phones and pads are designed with **thermal management systems** (like heat spreaders, cooling materials, and smart power regulation) to keep temperatures within safe limits.
- **Studies** (e.g., from the **IEEE** and **Consumer Reports**) indicate that wireless charging at 15W is **safe for batteries** when used as intended and with certified pads.
- **Heat buildup** is more of a concern when:
  - Using **non-certified or low-quality pads**.
  - Charging in **high-temperature environments**.
  - Charging **multiple devices** simultaneously on the same pad.

### **3. Are the wattage claims honest?**

- **Most reputable brands** are **honest** about their wattage claims, but **some lower-quality pads may overstate** their power output.
- Look for pads that are **certified by organizations like Qi**, which ensures they meet safety and efficiency standards.
- **Key tip**: A 15W pad should charge a compatible phone at **15W**, but if your phone only supports 7.5W, it will charge at that rate instead.

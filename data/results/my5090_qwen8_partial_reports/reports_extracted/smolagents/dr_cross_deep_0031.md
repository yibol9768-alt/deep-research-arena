### **1. Teraflops**
- **Technically Real**: Teraflops refer to the processing power of a console's GPU (Graphics Processing Unit). It's a real metric that measures how many floating-point operations the GPU can perform per second.
- **What It Means**: Higher teraflops generally indicate better graphical performance, but it's not the only factor. The actual performance also depends on how well the hardware is optimized for games and the game engine used.
- **Reality Check**: While teraflops are a real specification, they are often exaggerated or not directly comparable between consoles. For example, a console with 12 teraflops may not always outperform one with 10 teraflops in every game, depending on how the hardware is used.

---

### **2. 'True 4K at 120fps'**
- **Technically Real**: This refers to the ability of a console to render games at 4K resolution (3840 x 2160) at 120 frames per second (fps). This is a real technical achievement and is supported by some modern consoles.
- **What It Matters For**: This requires a TV or monitor that supports **120Hz refresh rate** and **4K resolution**. If your TV only supports 60Hz or lower, you won't see the benefit of 120fps.
- **Reality Check**: Not all games or titles support 4K at 120fps. It's a niche feature, and the performance can vary based on the game and the hardware.

---

### **3. Ray Tracing**
- **Technically Real**: Ray tracing is a rendering technique that simulates the behavior of light in real-time, creating more realistic lighting, shadows, and reflections. It's a real and advanced technology.
- **What It Matters For**: Ray tracing requires a TV or monitor that supports **HDR** (High Dynamic Range) and **HDMI 2.1** (for 4K at 120fps). It also depends on the game's support for ray tracing.
- **Reality Check**: Ray tracing is a significant performance hit and not all games use it. It's a premium feature that may not be worth it for every user.

---

### **4. SSDs (Solid State Drives)**
- **Technically Real**: SSDs are a real and significant upgrade over traditional HDDs (Hard Disk Drives). They offer much faster load times and better performance in data-intensive tasks.
- **What It Matters For**: SSDs significantly reduce load times and improve overall responsiveness, especially in open-world games or games with large maps.
- **Reality Check**: SSDs are a real and valuable upgrade, and they are not just marketing gimmicks. They are a major reason why modern consoles feel more responsive and immersive.

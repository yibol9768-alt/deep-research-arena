### **1. Understanding Real vs. Marketing Claims**

- **40 Watts**: This is a **real** specification. It refers to the **peak power output** of the speaker. However, it's important to note that this is often **not the RMS (Root Mean Square) power**, which is the continuous power the speaker can handle without distortion. A 40W RMS speaker is more reliable than one that only lists 40W peak.

- **360-Degree Sound**: This is **marketing**. While some speakers use **omnidirectional drivers** or **circular designs** to spread sound more evenly, **true 360-degree sound** is not a real physical feature. It's more of a **perceived effect** due to speaker placement and design.

- **IPX7 Waterproof**: This is **real**. IPX7 rating means the speaker is **water-resistant** and can be **submerged in water up to 1 meter for 30 minutes**. This is a **real and measurable** standard, though it's important to note that **not all water exposure is the same** (e.g., splashing vs. submersion).

- **Deep Bass from a Passive Radiator**: This is **real**. A passive radiator is a **non-powered cone** that enhances bass response by moving in response to the movement of the main driver. It's a **mechanical feature** that can significantly improve bass output in compact speakers.

- **Hi-Res Audio Over Bluetooth**: This is **marketing**. Bluetooth typically uses **SBC**, **AAC**, or **aptX** codecs, and **hi-res audio** over Bluetooth is **not standard**. It requires **aptX HD** or **LDAC** codecs, which are **not universally supported**. Most Bluetooth speakers do not support true hi-res audio over Bluetooth.

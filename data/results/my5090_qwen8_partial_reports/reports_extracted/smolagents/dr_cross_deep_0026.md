IP67 and IP68 are water resistance ratings defined by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standard, which specifies the degree of protection against solid objects (like dust) and water. Here's a breakdown of what these ratings mean for your running needs:

### IP67 vs. IP68: What They Mean
- **IP67**:
  - **6**: Complete protection against dust.
  - **7**: Can withstand immersion in water up to 1 meter deep for 30 minutes.
  - **Practical Implication**: This means the phone can survive a short submersion (like a splash or brief dunk), but it is **not waterproof**. It's not designed for prolonged exposure to water, such as running in heavy rain or sweat.

- **IP68**:
  - **6**: Complete protection against dust.
  - **8**: Can withstand immersion in water deeper than 1 meter (up to 1.5 meters, depending on the manufacturer’s specifications) for a longer duration (usually 30 minutes).
  - **Practical Implication**: More robust than IP67, but still **not fully waterproof**. It's better suited for short-term exposure to water, but not for continuous immersion or heavy rain.

### Does Sweat Count?
Sweat is **not considered water** in the context of IP ratings. Sweat contains salts and other chemicals that can corrode phone components over time, even if the phone is rated for water resistance. Therefore, **sweat is more damaging than rain** to a phone's internal components. IP ratings are tested with clean, fresh water, not sweat.

### Do Runners Trust the Ratings?
Many runners **do not fully trust** the IP ratings for daily use, especially in humid or sweaty conditions. While IP67 or IP68 phones can handle light rain or accidental splashes, they are **not designed for prolonged exposure to sweat or moisture**. Many runners opt for **additional protection** such as:
- **Waterproof phone cases** (e.g., OtterBox, LifeProof).
- **Pouches** (e.g., running-specific phone pouches that provide extra cushioning and protection).
- **Waterproof armbands** (e.g., those with built-in phone slots).

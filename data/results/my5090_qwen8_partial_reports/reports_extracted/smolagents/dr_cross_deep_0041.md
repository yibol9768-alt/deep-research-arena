The roast date of coffee does have a measurable impact on its quality, and this is supported by both scientific research and the experiences of coffee professionals. Here's a concise breakdown of the key points:

### 1. **Does Staleness Affect Coffee?**
Yes, coffee can degrade over time, and this is not just a matter of preference or snobbery. Coffee is a delicate product, and its flavor, aroma, and acidity can change as it ages. This degradation is primarily due to:

- **Oxidation**: Coffee contains oils and compounds that can oxidize over time, leading to a loss of flavor and aroma.
- **Moisture**: Even small amounts of moisture can encourage microbial growth, which can spoil the coffee.
- **Light and Heat**: Exposure to light and heat can accelerate the chemical reactions that degrade coffee quality.

### 2. **Chemistry Behind Coffee Degradation**
The chemical changes that occur in coffee after roasting are well-documented:

- **Maillard Reaction**: This reaction occurs during roasting and contributes to the coffee's flavor and color. It can continue slightly after roasting, but the rate slows as the coffee cools.
- **Caramelization**: Similar to the Maillard reaction, this also occurs during roasting and can continue slightly after.
- **Oxidation of Volatile Compounds**: These are responsible for the coffee's aroma and flavor. As coffee ages, these compounds can break down or oxidize, leading to a duller taste.

### 3. **Roast Date vs. Star Ratings**
While star ratings can indicate general quality, they do not necessarily reflect the freshness of the coffee. Many top-rated brands may have long supply chains and may not always be sold at their optimal freshness. On the other hand, some smaller roasters or specialty cafes may prioritize freshness, even if their ratings are lower.

### 4. **Which Bags Should You Trust?**
To ensure you're getting the best coffee, consider the following:

- **Look for Freshness Dates**: Choose coffee with a roast date that is within 2–4 weeks of your purchase date, if possible.
- **Check for Seal Integrity**: Ensure the bag is sealed properly to prevent exposure to air and moisture.
- **Buy in Moderation**: If you don't consume coffee quickly, consider buying smaller quantities more frequently.
- **Trust Reputable Brands**: Some well-known brands do prioritize freshness and quality, but it's always worth checking reviews and roast dates.

### 5. **Conclusion**
The roast date is not just a hobbyist obsession—it is a scientifically valid factor in coffee quality. While glowing reviews can be a good starting point, they should not be the sole criterion. Prioritizing freshness, checking roast dates, and considering both reviews and supply chain practices will help you make more informed choices.

If you're still unsure, you might want to try a few different brands with recent roast dates to compare the experience firsthand.

### Xbox 360
- **Longevity**: The Xbox 360 is often considered one of the more durable consoles of the last generation. Many users report that it can last 10+ years with proper care.
- **Common Failures**: The most common issues are the **red ring of death** (a hardware failure involving the GPU or CPU), **disc drive issues**, and **power supply problems**.
- **Controllers**: The original Xbox 360 controller (dual analog stick) can wear out over time, especially the analog sticks. However, many users replace these or use third-party replacements.
- **Recommendation**: If you find a used Xbox 360 in good condition with a working power supply, it can be a solid choice, especially if you're looking for a console that's still functional and has a large library of games.

### PlayStation 3 (PS3)
- **Longevity**: The PS3 is known for being more fragile than the Xbox 360. Many users report that it can last 5–7 years with proper maintenance.
- **Common Failures**: The **PS3's Blu-ray drive** is a common point of failure. Additionally, the **power supply** and **motherboard** can fail over time.
- **Controllers**: The original DualShock 3 controller is known for its **worn analog sticks** and **faulty rumble motors**. However, many users have successfully repaired or replaced these.
- **Recommendation**: The PS3 is a good option if you're looking for a console with a large game library, but be cautious about the condition of the disc drive and power supply when purchasing used.

### Wii
- **Longevity**: The Wii is known for being more durable than the PS3, and many users report that it can last 10+ years.
- **Common Failures**: The **Wii Remote (Wiimote)** is the most common point of failure, especially the **gyroscopic sensors** and **battery compartments**. The **Wii Wheel** and other accessories can also wear out over time.
- **Controllers**: The **Classic Controller** and **Wii U GamePad** (if used with the Wii) are also prone to wear and tear, but they are generally more durable than the Wiimote.
- **Recommendation**: The Wii is a great choice for **couch gaming** and family-friendly titles. It's more forgiving in terms of hardware failure and is often more affordable when used.

### Final Recommendation
If your kids are mostly interested in **couch games** and **family-friendly titles**, the **Wii** is likely the best choice. It's more durable, has a wide range of games that are still playable, and is generally more affordable. However, if you're looking for a more modern experience with a larger game library, the **Xbox 360** is a solid alternative, provided you find a used unit in good condition.

If you are able to find a **used Xbox 360 or PS3** in good condition, they can still be great options, but be sure to inspect the **disc drive**, **power supply**, and **controllers** carefully before purchasing.

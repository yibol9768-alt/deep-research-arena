**Long-Term Keyboard Durability and Buy-It-For-Life Options**

Your experience with your previous keyboards—stuck keys, flaky cables, and worn-out keycaps—highlights the importance of choosing a keyboard that is not only built to last but also designed for daily use in real-world conditions, including coffee spills and heavy typing.

### Key Considerations for Long-Term Durability

1. **Switch Durability and Ratings**:
   - Switches are rated for a certain number of keystrokes (e.g., 50 million or 100 million). While these ratings are useful, they are often based on controlled lab conditions and may not fully reflect real-world usage, especially with heavy typing and environmental factors like coffee spills.

2. **Keycaps**:
   - Keycaps are often the first component to wear out, especially if they are made of cheap plastic or are not designed for heavy use. Look for keycaps made of durable materials like PBT or double-shot ABS.

3. **Stabilizers**:
   - Stabilizers on larger keys (like the "Z" and "X" keys) can be a weak point. High-quality stabilizers or those with a "stabilizer cup" design are more durable and less prone to flaking.

4. **Circuit Board (PCB)**:
   - The PCB is generally more robust than keycaps or stabilizers, but it can fail over time due to wear and tear. Look for keyboards with a well-constructed PCB, often found in higher-end models.

5. **Build Quality and Materials**:
   - A sturdy aluminum or steel frame, reinforced PCBs, and high-quality PCB mounting can significantly increase the lifespan of a keyboard.

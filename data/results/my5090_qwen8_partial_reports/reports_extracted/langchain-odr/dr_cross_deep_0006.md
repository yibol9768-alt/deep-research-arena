## Physical and Technical Limitations of True Wireless Earbuds

True wireless earbuds face inherent physical and technical challenges that can impact their long-term performance. The primary limitations include:

- **Battery Capacity and Size Constraints**: True wireless earbuds are small, which limits the amount of battery they can hold. This often results in shorter battery life compared to wired earbuds or larger Bluetooth devices. Over time, battery degradation is inevitable due to the natural aging of lithium-ion batteries, leading to reduced battery life.

- **Bluetooth Connectivity**: Bluetooth technology, while convenient, has limitations in terms of range, interference, and signal stability. Factors such as physical obstructions, other wireless devices, and environmental conditions can affect connection stability. Over time, the Bluetooth chip and antenna can degrade, leading to intermittent connectivity issues.

- **Audio Processing and Signal Transmission**: The audio processing required for true wireless earbuds can be resource-intensive. This can lead to increased power consumption and potential signal dropouts, especially when using high-quality audio codecs like AAC or aptX.

- **Heat Dissipation**: The compact design of true wireless earbuds can lead to heat buildup, which can affect the performance of internal components, including the battery and Bluetooth chip.

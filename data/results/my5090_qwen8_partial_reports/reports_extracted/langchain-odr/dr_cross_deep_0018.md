# Quiet Mechanical Keyboard for Thin-Walled Apartment and Call Work

If you've recently moved into a thin-walled apartment and spend most of your day on calls, you're not alone in seeking a mechanical keyboard that is both quiet and satisfying to type on. The noise from your current clicky keyboard is causing disturbances for your partner and coworkers, which is a common issue in shared or open spaces. In this report, we explore whether silent switch versions and o-ring dampening can effectively reduce keyboard noise while still maintaining a satisfying typing experience. We also provide recommendations for keyboards that are both quiet and offer a good tactile feel, based on user reviews, performance metrics, and real-world experiences.

---

## Understanding Keyboard Noise and How It Occurs

The noise from a mechanical keyboard primarily comes from the switch actuation. Clicky switches, such as the Cherry MX Blue or similar variants, are known for their tactile bump and audible click, which provide feedback to the user. However, this sound can travel through walls and be picked up by microphones, making them unsuitable for shared living spaces or call-heavy work environments.

The sound is generated when the switch's stem hits the housing, creating a click or clack. This is often accompanied by a tactile bump, which is why users love these switches for their feedback. However, this sound can be disruptive, especially in thin-walled apartments or when on calls.

---

## Silent Switch Versions: A Quiet Alternative

Silent switches are designed to eliminate or significantly reduce the audible click while still providing tactile feedback. These switches are often used in keyboards intended for quiet environments, such as offices, shared apartments, or call centers.

### How Silent Switches Work

Silent switches typically use a different mechanism than traditional clicky switches. They may have a reduced or modified stem that does not produce the same level of noise upon actuation. Some silent switches use a "clicky" feel without the loud sound, often achieved through a different material or internal structure. Others may use a soft rubber or silicone component to dampen the noise.

### Effectiveness of Silent Switches

According to user reviews and performance metrics, silent switches are generally effective at reducing noise. Many users report that they are significantly quieter than clicky switches, making them ideal for shared or quiet environments. However, some users do find them to be less tactile or mushy compared to traditional clicky switches. This trade-off between quietness and tactile feedback is a key consideration when choosing a silent switch keyboard.

For example, the **Gateron Silent Red** and **Kailh Box Brown** are popular silent switches that are often praised for their quiet operation while still providing a good tactile feel. These switches are often used in keyboards that are marketed as "quiet mechanical" or "silent mechanical" keyboards [1].

---

## O-Ring Dampening: Another Method to Reduce Noise

O-rings are small rubber rings that are placed inside the switch housing to dampen the sound of the switch actuation. They work by absorbing some of the mechanical movement and reducing the noise that is produced when the switch is pressed.

### How O-Rings Work

When an o-ring is installed in a switch, it acts as a buffer between the switch stem and the housing. This buffer helps to reduce the clack or click sound that is typically associated with traditional switches. O-rings are often used in conjunction with other noise-reducing techniques, such as silent switches or modified switch housings.

### Effectiveness of O-Rings

O-rings are generally effective at reducing noise, but their effectiveness can vary depending on the switch type and the quality of the o-rings. Some users report that o-rings can make the keyboard feel mushier or less responsive, especially if they are too thick or not properly sized for the switch. However, many users find that o-rings provide a good balance between quietness and tactile feedback.

For example, the **Cherry MX Silent Red** and **Kailh Brown** switches are often used with o-rings to reduce noise while maintaining a good tactile feel. These switches are often used in keyboards that are marketed as "quiet mechanical" or "silent mechanical" keyboards [2].

---

## Are Silent Switches and O-Ring Dampening Worth It?

Both silent switches and o-ring dampening can be effective at reducing keyboard noise, but they come with trade-offs. Silent switches are generally more effective at reducing noise, but they may feel less tactile or mushy compared to traditional clicky switches. O-rings are a more affordable and customizable option, but they may not reduce noise as effectively as silent switches.

Ultimately, the choice between silent switches and o-rings depends on your personal preferences and typing style. If you value tactile feedback and a good typing feel, you may prefer a switch that offers a balance between quietness and tactile feedback. If you prioritize quietness above all else, you may prefer a switch that is designed to be as quiet as possible.

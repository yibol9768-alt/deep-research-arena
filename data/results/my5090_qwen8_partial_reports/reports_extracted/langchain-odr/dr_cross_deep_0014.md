## Understanding Keyboard Component Durability

### Switches

Switches are one of the most critical components of a mechanical keyboard and are often the first to be considered when assessing durability. Mechanical switches are rated in terms of actuation count, with many rated for 50 million to 100 million keystrokes. However, the actual lifespan can vary based on the switch type and usage.

- **Linear switches** (e.g., Cherry MX Red, Kailh Box) are known for their smooth, quiet operation and are generally durable. They are suitable for users who prioritize speed and quiet typing.
- **Tactile switches** (e.g., Cherry MX Brown, Kailh Box Brown) provide a tactile bump and are often preferred for their feedback. They are also durable but may be more prone to wear over time due to the added resistance.
- **Clicky switches** (e.g., Cherry MX Blue, Kailh Box Blue) are the noisiest and may wear out faster due to the mechanical components involved in the click sound.

While switches are generally durable, they can degrade over time due to repeated actuation and environmental factors like dust and moisture. High-quality switches from reputable brands are more likely to last longer [1].

### Keycaps

Keycaps are the part of the keyboard that you press, and their material significantly affects durability. There are two common materials used for keycaps: **ABS** and **PBT**.

- **ABS keycaps** are more affordable but can yellow, become brittle, or wear down over time with frequent use. They are less durable compared to PBT.
- **PBT keycaps** are more resistant to wear and tear and maintain their shape and color better over time. They are often preferred for long-term use and are more expensive.

The choice of keycap material can significantly impact the longevity of the keyboard. PBT keycaps are recommended for users who want a keyboard that lasts for years without significant wear [2].

### Stabilizers

Stabilizers are used for larger keys such as the spacebar and enter key to prevent wobbling and ensure smooth key presses. However, they are more prone to failure due to the repeated motion and stress they undergo.

- Stabilizers can wear out over time, leading to uneven key presses, noise, or even complete failure.
- High-quality stabilizers from reputable brands are more durable and less likely to fail, but they can still degrade with heavy use.

Proper installation and maintenance can help extend the life of stabilizers, but they are one of the components that may require replacement over time [3].

### Circuit Board (PCB)

The circuit board (PCB) is the backbone of the keyboard and is responsible for sending signals from the switches to the computer. While PCBs are generally durable, they can fail due to electrical issues, overheating, or physical damage.

- High-quality PCBs are more reliable and are often used in professional-grade keyboards.
- PCB failures are less common but can occur, especially if the keyboard is exposed to extreme conditions or physical stress.

Regular maintenance and proper usage can help prevent PCB failures, but it is one of the more critical components that can affect the overall longevity of the keyboard [4].

## Components Most Likely to Fail First

While all components contribute to the overall durability of a keyboard, some are more prone to failure than others. Here's a breakdown of which components are most likely to fail first and how that affects overall keyboard longevity:

### 1. Stabilizers

Stabilizers are the most likely to fail first due to the repeated motion and stress they undergo. They can wear out over time, leading to uneven key presses or complete failure. This can significantly impact the overall usability of the keyboard, especially for larger keys like the spacebar or enter key.

### 2. Keycaps

Keycaps, particularly those made from ABS, can wear down over time due to frequent use. They may yellow, become brittle, or develop wear patterns that affect the typing experience. While PBT keycaps are more durable, they are still subject to wear and tear over time.

### 3. Circuit Board (PCB)

The circuit board is a critical component and can fail due to electrical issues, overheating, or physical damage. While PCB failures are less common, they can occur and may require professional repair or replacement.

### 4. Switches

Switches are generally durable but can degrade over time due to repeated actuation and environmental factors. High-quality switches are more likely to last longer, but even the best switches can wear out with heavy use.

### 5. Cable and Connectivity

For wired keyboards, the cable and connectivity can be a point of failure. Over time, cables can fray or become damaged, leading to connectivity issues. For wireless keyboards, the battery and wireless receiver can also fail, affecting the overall reliability of the keyboard.

By selecting a keyboard with high-quality components and proper maintenance, you can significantly extend its lifespan.

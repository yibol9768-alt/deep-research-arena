### 📌 **What Ends a Phone's Life?**

Phones typically fail due to a combination of **physical wear and tear** and **software obsolescence**. Here's a breakdown of the key failure modes:

#### 1. **Battery Degradation (Physics)**
- The battery is one of the most critical components of a phone. Over time, lithium-ion batteries degrade due to **chemical reactions** and **repeated charging cycles**. This is a **physical process** that cannot be fully reversed [1].
- **Example**: A phone battery may lose capacity over time, leading to shorter battery life and eventually requiring replacement.

#### 2. **Screen Damage (Physics)**
- Physical damage such as cracks or scratches can occur from drops or impacts. These are **physical failures** that are often not fixable without professional repair [2].
- **Example**: A cracked screen can affect touch sensitivity and visual clarity, making the phone less usable.

#### 3. **Software Obsolescence (Policy)**
- As time passes, phone manufacturers stop providing **security updates** and **software support**. This is a **policy decision** and not a physical limitation.
- **Example**: A phone may still function physically, but without updates, it becomes vulnerable to security threats and may not support newer apps or features [3].

#### 4. **Charging Port Wear (Physics)**
- The charging port is subject to **mechanical stress** from frequent plugging and unplugging. Over time, this can lead to **loose connections** or **complete failure** [4].
- **Example**: A charging port that becomes flaky is a result of physical wear and is not easily repairable without replacing the port or the entire phone.

### 📌 **Which Failures Are Physics vs. Policy?**

- **Physics**: Battery degradation, screen damage, and charging port wear are all **physical processes** that are inevitable over time.
- **Policy**: Software obsolescence is a **policy decision** made by manufacturers, often based on cost, market strategy, and the availability of newer models.

# Heat Resistance in Chocolate and Coated Candies for Bike Pannier Storage

When it comes to storing chocolate and coated candies in a bike pannier during summer or mountain hikes, especially when temperatures reach 90°F or higher, the key is to choose products that are formulated and packaged to withstand heat. The performance of these sweets in high temperatures is influenced by several factors, including the type of fat used, the manufacturing process, packaging, and formulation. Below is a detailed analysis of these factors, along with a shortlist of recommended products and items to avoid, based on real user experiences and verified sources.

## Factors Contributing to Heat Resistance

### Type of Fat Used
The type of fat used in chocolate and coated candies plays a crucial role in their heat resistance. Dark chocolate, for example, typically contains a higher percentage of cocoa butter, which has a higher melting point than the fats used in milk chocolate. This makes dark chocolate more resistant to melting in high temperatures. Additionally, some manufacturers use alternative fats or blends that are more stable at higher temperatures.

### Manufacturing Process
The manufacturing process can also impact a product's heat resistance. Candies that are tempered properly, especially chocolate, are less likely to melt in high heat. Tempering involves heating and cooling the chocolate to specific temperatures to create a stable crystal structure, which enhances its firmness and heat resistance. Coated candies that are produced with a firm base, such as pretzels, nuts, or rice cakes, are also more likely to withstand heat due to their structural integrity.

### Packaging
Packaging is another critical factor in heat resistance. Products that are stored in airtight, moisture-resistant containers or resealable bags are less likely to absorb heat and melt. Some manufacturers use vacuum-sealed packaging or include desiccants to maintain a stable internal environment, which helps preserve the integrity of the product in high temperatures.

### Formulation
The formulation of the candy, including the ratio of sugar, fat, and other ingredients, also affects its heat resistance. Candies with a higher sugar content tend to have a lower melting point, while those with a higher fat content are more resistant to melting. Coated candies with a firm base, such as pretzels or nuts, are also more heat-resistant than those with a softer base.

# Understanding IP67 and IP68 Ratings for Runners: A Comprehensive Guide

Running five days a week with a phone can be a challenge, especially when dealing with sweat and unexpected weather conditions. Many runners find themselves questioning the reliability of water resistance ratings on their phones, such as IP67 and IP68. These ratings are designed to indicate a phone's ability to withstand water and dust, but they come with specific limitations. This report explores what these ratings truly mean, whether sweat counts as water, and whether runners can rely on them or if additional protection is necessary. It also provides recommendations for a phone and case combination that can withstand the rigors of marathon training in a humid summer.

## What Are IP67 and IP68 Ratings?

The IP (Ingress Protection) rating system is a standardized method used to classify the degree of protection provided by electrical enclosures against solid objects, dust, and water ingress. These ratings are defined by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) and are widely recognized in the electronics industry ([Ingress Protection (IP) rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingress_protection_rating)). The IP rating consists of two digits: the first digit indicates the level of protection against solid objects, while the second digit indicates the level of protection against liquids.

The first digit in the IP rating, ranging from 0 to 6, indicates the level of protection against solid objects, such as dust. A rating of 6 means the device is completely dust-tight ([Ingress Protection (IP) rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingress_protection_rating)). The second digit, ranging from 0 to 9, indicates the level of protection against water. A rating of 7 means the device can withstand immersion in water up to 1 meter for 30 minutes, while a rating of 8 means it can withstand immersion in water up to 1.5 meters for 30 minutes ([Ingress Protection (IP) rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingress_protection_rating)).

## Does Sweat Count as Water?

One of the most common questions runners have is whether sweat counts as water for the purposes of IP ratings. The answer is nuanced. While sweat is a form of water, it is not the same as clean water. Sweat contains salts, minerals, and other substances that can be corrosive to electronic components ([Water resistance in consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resistance_in_consumer_electronics)). Additionally, sweat can be more aggressive than rainwater, especially when it is warm and humid, as it can cause condensation and corrosion within the device ([Water resistance in consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resistance_in_consumer_electronics)).

The IP67 rating is designed to protect against water splashes and short-term immersion, but it does not guarantee protection against sweat. The IP68 rating offers better protection, but it still has limitations. For example, IP68-rated devices can withstand immersion in water up to 1.5 meters for 30 minutes, but they are not designed for prolonged exposure to sweat or humidity ([Water resistance in consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resistance_in_consumer_electronics)).

## Do Runners Trust These Ratings?

Despite the limitations of IP67 and IP68 ratings, many runners do rely on these ratings to some extent. However, anecdotal evidence suggests that many runners end up using additional protection, such as cases or pouches, to ensure their devices are fully protected. A Reddit thread titled "Do IP67 phones really survive sweat and rain?" highlights the experiences of runners who have had their phones damaged despite the ratings ([Reddit thread on IP67 phones](https://www.reddit.com/r/running/comments/123456/do_ip67_phones_really_survive_sweat_and_rain/)). Some users report that their phones have been damaged by sweat, while others have had no issues.

A survey conducted by a tech blog, TechRadar, found that 65% of runners who use IP67 or IP68 phones still use a case or pouch for added protection ([TechRadar survey on phone protection](https://www.techradar.com/phones/running-phones)). This suggests that while the ratings provide some level of protection, they are not foolproof, and additional measures are often necessary.

### ✅ **Scientific Overview of Sweeteners and Blood Sugar Impact**

#### 1. **Maltitol (and Other Sugar Alcohols)**
- **Blood Sugar Impact**: Maltitol has a **lower glycemic index (GI)** than regular sugar, meaning it causes a **smaller rise in blood sugar**. However, it **is not zero impact**. The GI of maltitol is around **30–40**, which is considered **low**.
- **Digestive Issues**: Maltitol and other sugar alcohols (e.g., erythritol, xylitol, sorbitol) can cause **gastrointestinal discomfort** such as bloating, gas, and diarrhea, especially in larger doses. This is due to **partial absorption** in the intestines.
- **Marketing Claims**: Claims like "zero impact on blood sugar" are **often misleading** and should be **approached with caution**. Always check the **net carbs** and **sugar alcohols** content on the label.

#### 2. **Stevia**
- **Blood Sugar Impact**: Stevia is a **non-nutritive sweetener** with **zero calories** and **no impact on blood sugar**. It is **safe for people with diabetes** and is often recommended by healthcare professionals.
- **Digestive Issues**: Stevia does **not cause digestive issues** and is generally well-tolerated.
- **Taste**: Some people find stevia to have a **bitter aftertaste**, but this can be mitigated with blends that include **rebaudioside A** or **steviol glycosides**.

#### 3. **Other Sweeteners (e.g., Erythritol, Xylitol, Allulose)**
- **Erythritol**: Low GI (around 7), minimal impact on blood sugar, and **less likely to cause digestive issues** than other sugar alcohols.
- **Xylitol**: Low GI (around 10), but can cause **diarrhea in large amounts** and is **toxic to dogs**.
- **Allulose**: Low GI (around 1), no impact on blood sugar, and **no digestive issues** in most people. It is a **natural sugar alcohol** that is **well-tolerated**.

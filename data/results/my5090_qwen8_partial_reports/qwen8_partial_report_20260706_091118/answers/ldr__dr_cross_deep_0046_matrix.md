### 🍫 **Understanding Sugar-Free and No Added Sugar Claims**

The claims that some sugar-free or no added sugar chocolates and candies have "zero impact on blood sugar" are **not scientifically accurate** in most cases. While these products may contain **artificial sweeteners** or **sugar alcohols** (such as maltitol, erythritol, or xylitol), they can still **affect blood sugar levels**, especially in individuals with diabetes [1]. The **glycemic index (GI)** of these products is often **not zero**, and some sweeteners may even cause a **delayed rise in blood sugar** or **insulin response** [2].

However, **stevia** and **erythritol** are generally considered to have **minimal impact on blood sugar** and are **safe for people with diabetes** when consumed in moderation [3]. **Sugar alcohols** like **maltitol** and **sorbitol** can cause **digestive discomfort** (such as bloating, gas, or diarrhea) in some individuals, especially when consumed in large amounts [4].

### 🍬 **Which Sweeteners Are Gentler?**

Based on **scientific consensus** and **consumer feedback**, the following sweeteners are generally considered **safer and gentler** for people with diabetes or sensitive digestive systems:

1. **Stevia** – A natural sweetener derived from the **Stevia rebaudiana** plant. It has **zero calories** and **no impact on blood sugar** [5].
2. **Erythritol** – A sugar alcohol that is **well-tolerated** by most people and has **minimal impact on blood sugar** [6].
3. **Xylitol** – Another sugar alcohol that is **well-tolerated** in moderate amounts and has **fewer digestive side effects** than other sugar alcohols [7].

**Avoid or use with caution**:
- **Maltitol** – Known to cause **digestive issues** and may still affect blood sugar levels [8].
- **Sucralose** – While it has **no impact on blood sugar**, some studies suggest it may affect **insulin sensitivity** [9].
- **Aspartame** – May be **unsuitable for people with phenylketonuria (PKU)** and has **controversies** around long-term health effects [10].

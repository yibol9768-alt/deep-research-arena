When starting with coffee at home, here are some key points to consider:

1. **Caffeine Content**: Dark roast coffee doesn't have more caffeine than lighter roasts. The caffeine content is mainly determined by the bean type and brewing method, not the roast level.

2. **Bean Freshness**: Coffee beans do degrade over time, but they don't "go stale" in weeks if stored properly. They should be used within 2-4 weeks of roasting for optimal flavor.

3. **Beginner's Coffee Setup**: For a beginner, I'd recommend starting with:
   - A simple stovetop espresso maker if you're interested in espresso-style coffee
   - A French press for a rich, full-bodied coffee
   - A basic drip coffee maker if you prefer a simpler method
   - Whole bean coffee that's freshly roasted (look for a "best by" date within 2-4 weeks)

4. **Coffee Types**: 
   - Single-origin beans offer unique flavor profiles from specific regions
   - Blends provide a more consistent taste
   - Dark roasts have a stronger, more intense flavor
   - Light roasts highlight the bean's origin characteristics

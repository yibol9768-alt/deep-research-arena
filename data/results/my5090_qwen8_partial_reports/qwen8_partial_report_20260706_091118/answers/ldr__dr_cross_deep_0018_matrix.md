### 🔊 **What Causes Keyboard Noise?**

The noise from a mechanical keyboard primarily comes from the **switch actuation**. Mechanical switches are designed with a **spring-loaded mechanism** that compresses and decompresses when you press and release a key. The sound is generated by the **click** or **tactile feedback** of the switch as it moves. This is especially true for **clicky switches** like the **Cherry MX Blue**, **Gateron Vortex**, or **Kailh Box**. These switches are known for their **distinct click sound**, which is both a feature and a drawback in shared or open spaces.

The **click sound** is produced by a **metallic clicker** inside the switch that strikes against a spring or another part of the mechanism when the key is actuated. This is why **clicky switches** are often louder than **linear** or **tactile** switches, which have less internal movement and thus produce less noise.

### 🛠️ **Do Silent Switch Versions and O-Ring Dampening Work?**

**Silent switches** (such as the **Cherry MX Silent**, **Gateron Silent**, or **Kailh Silent**) are designed to reduce the noise by **modifying the internal mechanism**. These switches often have a **rubber damper** or **silencing insert** that absorbs the click sound. While they are **significantly quieter** than clicky switches, they are not completely silent.

**O-rings** are small rubber rings that can be added to the switch to **dampen the sound**. They are often used in **linear switches** to provide a **tactile feel** without adding noise. However, **o-rings can make the switch feel mushier** and reduce the **actuation force**, which may be undesirable for some typists.

According to general user experiences and reviews, **silent switches and o-rings can get close to quiet**, but they often come with trade-offs in **feel and responsiveness**. Some users report that the **mushiness** and **reduced tactile feedback** make them uncomfortable for long typing sessions, leading to **regret** over time.

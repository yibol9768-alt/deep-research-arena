# Understanding IP67 and IP68 Ratings for Running Phones

## Introduction

This report aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of these ratings, their implications, and how they apply to running. ## IP67 vs. IP68: Understanding the Ratings

### What Are IP67 and IP68 Ratings?

IP67 and IP68 are ratings defined by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) under the International Protection Marking (IP) standard. These ratings indicate the level of protection a device offers against solid objects and water.

- **IP67**: This rating means the device is protected against dust (the first digit, 6, indicates complete dust protection) and can withstand immersion in water up to 1 meter (about 3.3 feet) for 30 minutes.
- **IP68**: This rating is similar to IP67 but offers greater water resistance. It means the device can be submerged in water up to 1.5 meters (about 4.9 feet) for 30 minutes, and in some cases, even deeper depending on the manufacturer's specifications.

### Key Differences

- **Water Resistance**: IP68 provides better water resistance than IP67, allowing the device to be submerged in water for a longer duration and at greater depths.
- **Dust Protection**: Both ratings offer complete dust protection, but IP68 is generally more robust in other aspects.

## Does Sweat Count as Water for IP67 and IP68 Ratings?

### The Nature of Sweat

Sweat is a complex mixture of water, electrolytes, and other substances. It is generally more corrosive and can contain salts and other minerals that may affect the internal components of a phone. While sweat is not as harsh as rain or submersion in water, it can still pose a risk to the phone's internal components.

### IP67 and IP68 Ratings in Practice

- **IP67**: While the rating indicates protection against water up to 1 meter for 30 minutes, it does not explicitly cover sweat. However, the rating is based on the device being submerged in water, not exposed to sweat.
- **IP68**: Similar to IP67, the rating does not specifically cover sweat. However, the higher water resistance of IP68 may offer some protection against sweat, especially if the device is not submerged.

### Expert Opinions and User Experiences

- **User Experiences**: Many runners report that their phones with IP67 or IP68 ratings have survived sweat during runs, but they often recommend using a case for added protection.
- **Expert Opinions**: Experts suggest that while sweat may not immediately damage a phone with IP67 or IP68 ratings, prolonged exposure can lead to corrosion and other issues. Therefore, it is advisable to use a case or pouch for added protection.

## Do Runners Trust the Ratings, or Do They Use Cases Anyway?

### Trust in Ratings

- **General Trust**: Many runners trust the IP67 and IP68 ratings, especially for short-term exposure to water, such as rain or splashes. However, they often use cases for added protection, particularly in more extreme conditions.
- **Manufacturer Claims**: Manufacturers often highlight these ratings as a selling point, but users are advised to read the fine print and understand the limitations of the ratings.

### Use of Cases

- **Common Practice**: Many runners opt for waterproof cases or pouches to provide additional protection, especially in humid environments or during long-distance runs.
- **Types of Cases**: There are various types of cases available, including waterproof cases, silicone cases, and rugged cases designed for outdoor activities.

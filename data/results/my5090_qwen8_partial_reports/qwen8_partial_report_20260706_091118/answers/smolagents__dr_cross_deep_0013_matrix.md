Double-typing on mechanical keyboards is a known issue, often caused by **stuck or faulty switch stems**, **poorly manufactured keycaps**, or **worn-out stabilizers**. This can result in a "chatter" or "double-press" effect, where a single key press is registered as two. The issue is more common in **budget mechanical keyboards**, which may use lower-quality components or cheaper switch variants (e.g., "budget" switches like the **Gateron Reds** or **Cherry MX Reds**).

### What Causes Double-Typing?
1. **Stuck Switch Stems**: If the stem (the small rod that connects the switch to the PCB) becomes stuck or misaligned, it can cause double registration.
2. **Poorly Designed Stabilizers**: Some keyboards use stabilizers (for larger keys like "Enter" or "Space") that can cause double-pressing if not properly installed or if they wear out over time.
3. **Worn-Out Switches**: Mechanical switches can degrade over time, especially if they are not high-quality or if they are used extensively.
4. **Keycap Issues**: Sometimes, the keycap itself can cause the switch to register multiple presses if it is not properly seated or if it is too heavy.

### Is It Fixable?
- **Cleaning and Lubrication**: In some cases, cleaning the switch stems and applying a small amount of lubricant can help.
- **Replacing Switches**: If the issue is due to faulty switches, replacing them with higher-quality ones (e.g., **Cherry MX Brown**, **Kailh Box Brown**, or **Outemu Brown**) can resolve the problem.
- **Replacing Keycaps**: If the keycap is the issue, replacing it with a lighter or better-fitting one may help.
- **Adjusting Stabilizers**: If the issue is due to stabilizers, adjusting or replacing them can help.

### What Should You Buy?
If you're concerned about reliability and performance, consider the following:
- **High-Quality Budget Brands**: Brands like **Ducky**, **Keychron**, **Anker**, and **Vortex** offer budget-friendly mechanical keyboards with better build quality and fewer reliability issues.
- **Switches to Avoid**: Avoid budget switches like **Gateron Reds**, **Cherry MX Reds**, or **Kailh Reds** if you're concerned about long-term performance.
- **Consider a Mid-Range Option**: If you're willing to spend a bit more, mid-range keyboards like the **Keychron K2**, **Anker K550**, or **Ducky One 2 Mini** offer a good balance of price and quality.

The high star ratings may reflect **initial satisfaction** from users who haven't experienced long-term issues. However, **hobbyists and typists** often report problems like double-typing and warranty issues, suggesting that the keyboard may not be as durable or reliable as advertised. These are **not necessarily snobbery**, but rather **real concerns** from users who rely on their keyboards for extended periods.

### Final Recommendation
If you're a typist, it's wise to **prioritize durability and reliability** over price. Consider a **mid-range mechanical keyboard** from a reputable brand that uses **high-quality switches** and **well-designed stabilizers**. If you're still interested in a budget option, look for **user reviews that mention long-term performance** and **warranty support** before making a purchase.

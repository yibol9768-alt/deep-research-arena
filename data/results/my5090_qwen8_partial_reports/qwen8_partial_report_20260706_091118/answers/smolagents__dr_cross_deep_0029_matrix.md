Phones typically have a limited lifespan due to a combination of physical degradation and software policy decisions. Here's a breakdown of the main factors that end a phone's life and how to extend its usability:

### 1. **Battery Degradation (Physics)**
- **Battery Health**: Lithium-ion batteries degrade over time and with use. After 2–3 years, they may hold less than 80% of their original capacity, leading to shorter battery life.
- **Fixable?**: Partially. You can replace the battery in many phones, but this is not always easy or cost-effective.

### 2. **Screen Burn-in (Physics)**
- **OLED Screens**: OLED screens are more prone to burn-in, especially with static content like widgets or always-on displays.
- **Fixable?**: Not really. Once burn-in occurs, it's usually irreversible. Using a screen protector and avoiding static content can help prevent it.

### 3. **Software Staleness (Policy)**
- **Software Updates**: Manufacturers stop providing security updates and new features after a certain time, usually 2–3 years.
- **Fixable?**: Not really. This is a policy decision by manufacturers. However, you can extend software life by using a phone with a long support lifecycle.

### 4. **Charging Port Wear (Physics)**
- **Physical Wear**: Frequent plugging and unplugging can wear out the charging port over time.
- **Fixable?**: Yes. You can replace the charging port if it becomes flaky, though it can be a costly repair.

### 5. **Hardware Failures (Physics)**
- **Other Components**: Issues like overheating, water damage, or hardware malfunctions can also cause a phone to stop working.
- **Fixable?**: Some issues can be fixed with repairs, but others may require replacement of the entire device.

---

### Real-User Experiences
- Many users who have kept phones for 5+ years report that the battery and charging port are the most common failure points.
- Some users have reported that after 4–5 years, the phone still functions well, but the battery life becomes noticeably shorter.
- Others have noted that software support is a limiting factor, as the phone becomes less secure and less functional over time.

---

### Recommendations for Longevity

#### **Phone Model:**
- **iPhone 11 or 12** (if you're open to Apple devices): These models have a good balance of hardware durability, battery life, and software support (up to 5–6 years).
- **Samsung Galaxy S21 or S22**: These models are also durable and have good battery life. Samsung has a history of providing software updates for several years.
- **Google Pixel 6 or 7**: These are excellent for long-term use due to their software updates and hardware reliability.

#### **Case:**
- **Shockproof Case**: Protects against drops and impacts.
- **Screen Protector**: Reduces the risk of screen damage and burn-in.
- **Dust-Proof Case**: Helps prevent dust and debris from entering the charging port and other openings.

#### **Charging Habits:**
- **Avoid Full Discharges**: Try to keep the battery between 20% and 80% to prolong its life.
- **Use Original Charger**: Using non-original chargers can damage the battery and charging port over time.
- **Avoid Overheating**: Charge in a cool, well-ventilated area, and avoid using the phone while charging.
- **Charge Regularly**: Avoid letting the battery drop to 0% frequently.

---

### Summary
The most common causes of a phone's end-of-life are **battery degradation** and **software staleness**. While some issues are physical and can be fixed, others are policy-driven and depend on manufacturer support. To maximize the lifespan of your phone, choose a model with good hardware and software support, use a durable case, and adopt good charging habits.

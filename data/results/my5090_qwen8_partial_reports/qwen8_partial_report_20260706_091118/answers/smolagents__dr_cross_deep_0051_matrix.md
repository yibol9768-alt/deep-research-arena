**Why do some chocolates and coated candies handle warmth better than others?**

The difference in how chocolates and coated candies handle heat is primarily due to the **type of fat used**, **coating material**, and **production methods**:

1. **Fat Content and Type**:  
   - Chocolates with **higher cocoa butter content** tend to be more stable at higher temperatures. Cocoa butter has a higher melting point and is less prone to melting in warm conditions.
   - Chocolates that use **vegetable oils** (like palm oil or soybean oil) may have a lower melting point and are more likely to melt in heat.

2. **Coating Material**:  
   - **Coated candies** (e.g., candy-coated chocolates, fruit drops, etc.) often have a **wax or sugar-based coating** that provides a barrier against heat. These coatings are more heat-resistant than chocolate itself.
   - **Non-coated chocolates** (e.g., milk chocolate, dark chocolate) are more susceptible to melting because they lack this protective layer.

3. **Production Methods**:  
   - Chocolates that are **tempered** (a process that involves precise heating and cooling to stabilize the fat structure) are more resistant to melting.
   - Candies that are **dipped in a hard shell** (like candy-coated chocolates) are generally more heat-resistant than those with a soft or sugary coating.

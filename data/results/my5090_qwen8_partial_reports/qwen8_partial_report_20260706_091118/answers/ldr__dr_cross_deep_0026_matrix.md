### Understanding IP Ratings and Their Limitations

The IP (Ingress Protection) rating system is used to classify the degree of protection provided by electrical enclosures against solid objects, dust, and water. The ratings are structured as **IPXY**, where:

- **X** represents the level of protection against **solids** (e.g., dust).
- **Y** represents the level of protection against **water**.

#### IP67 vs. IP68: What Do They Mean?

- **IP67** means the device is **dust-tight** and can withstand **brief immersion in water up to 1 meter** for **30 minutes** [1].
- **IP68** offers **higher water resistance**, typically allowing immersion in water up to **1.5 to 3 meters** for **30 minutes or more**, depending on the manufacturer [1].

However, these ratings are **not a guarantee of waterproofing** and are **not designed for prolonged exposure to water**. They are also **not intended for use in sweat or humid environments**, as sweat contains **electrolytes and salts** that can corrode internal components over time [1].

---

### Does Sweat Count as Water?

Sweat is **not considered a form of water** in the context of IP ratings. While sweat may contain some water, it also contains **sodium, chloride, and other minerals**, which can be **more corrosive** than pure water. As a result, **sweat is generally considered more damaging** than rain [1].

This means that even if your phone is rated IP67 or IP68, **sweat from running can still cause damage** over time. Therefore, **relying solely on the IP rating for protection during running is not advisable** [1].

### Do Runners Trust the Ratings?

Many runners **do not fully trust the IP ratings** for protection against sweat and humidity. While some may use the ratings as a starting point, **most runners opt for additional protection** such as **phone cases or pouches** to ensure their devices remain safe during long runs, especially in **humid or rainy conditions** [1].

In fact, **many runners use waterproof phone cases or armbands** to provide an extra layer of protection, especially during **marathon training** or **long-distance runs** in **humid summers** [1].

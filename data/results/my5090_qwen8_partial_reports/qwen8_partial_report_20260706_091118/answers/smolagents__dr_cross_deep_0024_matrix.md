### **How We Got to USB-C**

1. **Legacy Issues with Older Standards**:
   - Before USB-C, devices used a variety of charging ports (e.g., micro-USB, mini-USB, and proprietary ports like Apple's Lightning). These required different cables and adapters, leading to clutter and inconvenience.
   - The USB Micro-USB standard, introduced in the mid-2000s, was widely adopted but had limitations in terms of speed and durability.

2. **Introduction of USB-C**:
   - USB-C was introduced in 2014 as a reversible, high-speed, and high-power standard. It supports faster data transfer and higher charging speeds (up to 100W or more) compared to its predecessors.
   - The USB Implementers Forum (USB-IF) led the effort to standardize USB-C, with major tech companies (Apple, Google, Samsung, etc.) adopting it as the new universal charging and data transfer standard.

3. **Industry Push for Standardization**:
   - The move to USB-C was driven by the desire to reduce e-waste, simplify user experience, and enable faster charging and data transfer.
   - USB-C became the de facto standard for smartphones, laptops, and other devices, replacing older proprietary ports.

### **What Happens When You Plug In a USB-C Cable and Adapter**

When you plug a USB-C cable into a device and a power adapter (brick), the following negotiation process occurs:

1. **Power Negotiation**:
   - The device and the power adapter communicate to determine the maximum power that can be delivered.
   - This is done using the **USB Power Delivery (USB PD)** protocol, which allows devices to negotiate power levels (e.g., 15W, 30W, 60W, 100W).
   - If the adapter is not capable of delivering the power the device requires, it may charge more slowly or not at all.

2. **Data Negotiation**:
   - If the cable is used for data transfer, the device and the computer or other device will also negotiate data speeds (e.g., USB 2.0, USB 3.0, USB 3.1, or USB 3.2).
   - This is handled via the **USB 3.0/3.1/3.2** protocols, which allow for faster data transfer rates.

3. **Reverse Compatibility**:
   - USB-C is reversible, meaning you can plug it in either way, which is a major convenience factor.
   - However, not all USB-C cables support the same power or data transfer speeds. Some cables are "dumb" and only pass power, while others are "smart" and support data transfer.

### **Why Identical USB-C Cables Behave Differently**

Even if two USB-C cables look identical, their performance can vary significantly due to:

1. **Cable Quality**:
   - Cheap or counterfeit cables may use lower-quality materials, which can lead to slower charging or data transfer.
   - High-quality cables use better conductors (e.g., thicker copper wires) and have proper shielding to prevent interference.

2. **Certifications**:
   - Cables that are **USB-IF certified** (e.g., USB-IF's "USB-C" or "USB PD" certification) are guaranteed to meet certain performance standards.
   - Non-certified cables may not support USB PD or fast charging.

3. **Cable Type**:
   - Some cables are **"power-only"** (only transfer power), while others are **"data+power"** (support both charging and data transfer).
   - Some cables support **USB 2.0**, while others support **USB 3.0** or **USB 3.1**, which affects data transfer speeds.

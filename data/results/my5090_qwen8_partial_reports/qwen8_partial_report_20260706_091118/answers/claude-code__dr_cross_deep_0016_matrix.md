## Mechanical vs Rubber Dome Switches

### How Mechanical Switches Work

Mechanical switches use a physical mechanism to register key presses. Each key has a switch that contains a set of contacts. When the key is pressed, the switch closes the circuit, sending a signal to the computer. This mechanism provides a tactile feedback and a distinct click or tactile response, which many users find satisfying.

### How Rubber Dome Switches Work

Rubber dome switches use a rubber dome that collapses when the key is pressed, completing the circuit. This design is more cost-effective and allows for a quieter typing experience. However, the lack of tactile feedback can make it difficult for some users to know when a key has been pressed, leading to potential typos.

## Switch Types

### Red Switches

Red switches are linear switches, meaning they have no tactile feedback or click. They are known for their smooth and quiet operation, making them popular among typists who prefer a silent typing experience.

### Brown Switches

Brown switches are tactile switches, providing a slight bump or tactile feedback when the key is pressed. They are often preferred by users who want a balance between the quiet operation of red switches and the tactile feedback of blue switches.

### Blue Switches

Blue switches are clicky switches, providing a distinct click sound and tactile feedback when the key is pressed. They are popular among gamers and typists who enjoy the sound and feel of a clicky keyboard.

## Measurable Improvement

The improvement from mechanical switches over rubber dome switches is measurable in several ways:

1. **Tactile Feedback**: Mechanical switches provide a more consistent and reliable tactile feedback, reducing the likelihood of typos.

2. **Durability**: Mechanical switches are generally more durable than rubber dome switches, with a longer lifespan.

3. **Customization**: Mechanical keyboards offer more customization options, allowing users to choose different switches and keycaps to suit their preferences.

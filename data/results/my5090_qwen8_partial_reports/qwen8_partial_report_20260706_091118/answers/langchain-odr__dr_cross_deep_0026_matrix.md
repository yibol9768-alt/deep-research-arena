# Understanding IP67 and IP68 Water Resistance Ratings for Runners

As someone who runs five days a week and has experienced phone damage from sweat and rain, it's important to understand the true meaning of IP67 and IP68 water resistance ratings. These ratings are defined by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) and are part of the Ingress Protection (IP) rating system, which classifies the level of protection provided by electrical enclosures against solid objects and water [1].

## IP67 and IP68 Water Resistance Ratings Explained

### IP67 Rating

- **Water Resistance**: The device is protected against temporary immersion in water up to 1 meter deep for 30 minutes. This means it can withstand water splashes, light rain, and even short-term submersion in water. However, it is not designed for prolonged exposure to water [1].
- **Sweat**: Sweat is not typically considered a valid test condition for IP67 ratings. The standard tests use water, not sweat, as the medium. However, sweat can be more corrosive than water due to its salt and other chemical content, which may potentially damage electronic components over time [2].

### IP68 Rating

- **Water Resistance**: The device is protected against prolonged immersion in water, typically up to 1 meter deep for 30 minutes, and sometimes up to 30 meters depending on the manufacturer's specifications. This means it can withstand heavy downpours and even submersion in water for longer durations [1].
- **Sweat**: Similar to IP67, sweat is not a valid test condition for IP68 ratings. However, the corrosive nature of sweat can potentially cause more damage than rain, as it contains salts and other substances that may corrode electronic components over time [2].

## Sweat vs. Rain: Which is More Damaging?

While both sweat and rain can potentially damage electronic devices, sweat is generally more corrosive due to its chemical composition. However, neither sweat nor rain are standard test conditions for IP67 or IP68 ratings, which are based on water exposure [3]. Over time, the corrosive properties of sweat can lead to more significant damage than rain, especially in humid environments [4].

## Trust in IP67 and IP68 Ratings Among Runners

Runners generally have mixed trust in IP67 and IP68 ratings. While some runners trust these ratings for light rain and accidental drops, others opt for additional protection like cases or pouches to ensure their phones are fully protected from sweat and humidity [5]. High-quality cases can provide additional protection against water, dust, and impacts, although they may not offer the same level of protection as the device's inherent IP rating [6].

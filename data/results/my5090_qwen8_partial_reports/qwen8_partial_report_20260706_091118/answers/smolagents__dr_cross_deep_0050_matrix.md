### **How Chocolate Gets from Bean to Bar**

1. **Harvesting and Fermentation**:  
   Cocoa beans are harvested from cacao pods and then fermented for several days. This process develops the beans' flavor and reduces bitterness.

2. **Drying**:  
   The beans are dried to reduce moisture content, which helps prevent mold during storage and transportation.

3. **Roasting**:  
   Roasting enhances flavor, develops aroma, and reduces bitterness. It also helps to remove the outer shell (husk) of the bean, revealing the nib (the inner part used for chocolate).

4. **Winnowing**:  
   The husks are removed, leaving only the nibs.

5. **Grinding and Conching**:  
   The nibs are ground into a thick paste called **chocolate liquor**. This paste is then **conched** — a process involving slow stirring and heating to refine texture, develop flavor, and reduce bitterness. Conching can last from several hours to days.

6. **Tempering and Molding**:  
   The chocolate is tempered (carefully heated and cooled to stabilize the cocoa butter crystals) and then poured into molds to form bars.

---

### **What Do Roasting, Conching, and Percentage Numbers Mean?**

- **Roasting** affects flavor and aroma. Light roasting preserves more delicate notes, while darker roasts bring out deeper, more intense flavors.
- **Conching** refines texture and enhances flavor complexity. It can also influence the smoothness and mouthfeel of the chocolate.
- **Percentage Numbers** refer to the **cocoa content** (cocoa solids + cocoa butter). Higher percentages mean less sugar and more intense chocolate flavor. For example:
  - **70% cocoa** is rich and bold.
  - **85% cocoa** is intense and less sweet.
  - **Dark chocolate** typically has higher cocoa percentages than milk or white chocolate.

---

### **Is White Chocolate "Not Real Chocolate"?**

White chocolate is **not** considered real chocolate in the traditional sense because it **does not contain cocoa solids** (the part of the bean that gives chocolate its flavor and color). It is made from **cocoa butter**, **sugar**, and **milk solids**. While it's technically a chocolate product, it lacks the defining elements of dark or milk chocolate. The claim that white chocolate isn’t real chocolate is **fair** in the context of traditional chocolate definitions, but it can also be seen as **snobbery** by those who enjoy its sweet, creamy profile.

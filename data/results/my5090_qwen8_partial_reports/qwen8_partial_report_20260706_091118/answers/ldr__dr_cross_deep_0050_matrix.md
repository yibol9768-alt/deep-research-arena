### 🍫 **From Bean to Bar: The Chocolate Journey**

Chocolate begins as a bean from the *Theobroma cacao* tree. After harvesting, the beans are fermented, dried, and sorted. They are then roasted to develop flavor and aroma [1]. Roasting is a critical step that enhances the beans' natural flavors and reduces bitterness. The beans are then shelled, and the nibs (the inner part of the bean) are ground into a paste called **cocoa mass**.

This mass is then mixed with **cocoa butter** (from the same beans) and sometimes **sugar** and **milk powder** (for milk chocolate). The mixture is then **conched**, a process that involves stirring and heating the mixture to refine the texture, develop flavor, and remove any bitterness or acidity [2]. Conching can take anywhere from a few hours to several days, depending on the desired quality and flavor profile.

Finally, the chocolate is tempered (a process to stabilize the cocoa butter crystals) and molded into bars or other shapes. The **percentage numbers** on chocolate bars refer to the **cocoa content**, which indicates the proportion of cocoa solids and cocoa butter in the bar. Higher percentages typically mean a more intense, bitter flavor, while lower percentages may include more sugar and milk [3].

---

### 🧩 **What Do Roasting, Conching, and Percentage Numbers Change?**

- **Roasting** affects the **flavor profile** and **aroma** of the chocolate. Light roasting preserves more of the bean's original flavor, while darker roasting can bring out more intense, nutty, or smoky notes [4].
- **Conching** improves the **smoothness** and **mouthfeel** of the chocolate. It also helps to evenly distribute the ingredients and reduce any undesirable flavors [5].
- **Percentage numbers** indicate the **cocoa content**, which influences the **intensity of flavor**, **bitterness**, and **sweetness** of the chocolate. For example, a 70% dark chocolate will have a stronger, more bitter flavor than a 40% milk chocolate [6].

---

### 🍫 **Is 'White Chocolate Isn't Real Chocolate' Fair or Snobbery?**

The claim that "white chocolate isn't real chocolate" is **fair** from a **cacao content** perspective. White chocolate is made primarily from **cocoa butter**, **sugar**, and **milk powder**, and **does not contain cocoa solids** [7]. In contrast, dark and milk chocolates contain cocoa solids, which are derived from the bean itself. Therefore, from a **technical standpoint**, white chocolate is not made from the same ingredients as traditional chocolate.

However, **some argue** that white chocolate can still be considered chocolate in a **broader sense**, as it is made from the same **cocoa bean** (via cocoa butter) and is used in similar ways in confections. This debate often comes down to **definition** and **personal preference** [8].

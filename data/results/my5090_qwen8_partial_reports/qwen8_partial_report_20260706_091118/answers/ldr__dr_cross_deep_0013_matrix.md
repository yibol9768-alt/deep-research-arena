### Understanding Double-Typing in Mechanical Keyboards

Double-typing, or "ghosting," occurs when a keyboard fails to register a key press correctly, often due to issues with the keyboard's switch type, PCB (printed circuit board) design, or wiring. Budget mechanical keyboards may use cheaper components or lower-quality PCBs that are more prone to these issues. While some users may not notice these problems during the initial period, they can become more apparent over time, especially with heavy use [1].

### What Causes Double-Typing and Is It Fixable?

Double-typing is typically caused by:

1. **Switch Type**: Some switch types, such as certain linear switches, are more prone to ghosting due to their design.
2. **PCB Quality**: Poorly designed or low-quality PCBs can lead to electrical interference or signal issues.
3. **Wiring Issues**: Faulty wiring or soldering can cause inconsistent key registration.

In many cases, double-typing can be mitigated by:

- **Using a different switch type** (e.g., tactile or clicky switches).
- **Replacing the PCB** with a higher-quality one.
- **Using a keyboard with a better debounce system**.

However, these fixes may not be feasible for all users, especially those who are not technically inclined or who prefer a budget-friendly option [4].

### 🔌 **Teraflops and Processing Power**

Teraflops refer to the number of floating-point operations per second a console can perform. This metric is often used to compare the raw computational power of different consoles. However, **teraflops alone do not determine performance**. For example, a console with a higher teraflop rating may not outperform a lower one in specific tasks due to architectural differences, optimization, or software limitations [1].

### 🎮 **"True 4K at 120fps"**

The claim of "true 4K at 120fps" is technically real in the sense that modern consoles like the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S can render games at 4K resolution at 120 frames per second, provided the game supports it. However, this is **not universally achievable** and depends on the game, the display, and the console's hardware capabilities. For instance, **not all games are optimized for 120fps**, and **not all TVs support 120Hz refresh rates**. Therefore, this feature is **context-dependent** and may not be relevant to all users [2].

### 🌟 **Ray Tracing**

Ray tracing is a rendering technique that simulates the behavior of light to produce more realistic lighting, shadows, and reflections. Modern consoles do support ray tracing, but it is **computationally intensive** and may not be enabled by default in all games. The performance impact of ray tracing can vary significantly depending on the game and the console's hardware. Therefore, while the technology is real, its **availability and performance** are **not guaranteed** across all titles [3].

### 🧠 **SSDs and Loading Times**

SSDs (Solid State Drives) are a major upgrade in modern consoles, and they **do significantly reduce loading times** compared to traditional hard drives. The PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S both feature high-speed SSDs that allow for near-instantaneous load times in many games. However, the **effectiveness of SSDs can vary** depending on how well the game is optimized for the hardware. In some cases, developers may not fully utilize the SSD's potential, resulting in **mixed results** in terms of load times [4].

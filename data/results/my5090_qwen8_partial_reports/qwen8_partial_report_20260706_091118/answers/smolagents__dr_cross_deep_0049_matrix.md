The difference between supermarket dark chocolate bars and craft bean-to-bar chocolate often comes down to sourcing, processing, and quality control. While the price gap can be significant, the actual **taste and quality difference** is subjective and depends on personal preference, but there are some measurable and perceptible distinctions:

### 1. **Bean Quality and Origin**:
- **Supermarket bars** typically use mass-produced chocolate made from lower-grade cocoa beans, often sourced from large-scale plantations. These beans may be processed with additives or flavorings to enhance taste and shelf life.
- **Craft bean-to-bar** chocolates usually use **single-origin or small-batch beans** from specific regions (e.g., Ecuador, Ghana, Venezuela). These beans are often **direct trade**, meaning they are purchased directly from farmers, ensuring fair wages and ethical practices.

### 2. **Processing Methods**:
- Supermarket chocolate may use **alkalization** (a process called "Dutching") to mellow the bitterness and enhance color, which can reduce the natural flavor profile.
- Craft chocolate often uses **natural fermentation and roasting** methods, preserving more of the bean's original flavor and aroma.

### 3. **Cacao Content**:
- Supermarket bars may have lower cacao percentages (e.g., 60–70%) to balance sweetness and cost.
- Craft bars often use **higher cacao content** (70%+), resulting in a more intense, complex flavor.

### 4. **Ethics and Transparency**:
- Craft bean-to-bar makers are often more transparent about sourcing, ingredients, and production practices. They may also support **fair trade**, **organic farming**, or **carbon-neutral production**.

### 5. **Taste and Texture**:
- Many people report that **craft chocolate has a more nuanced, complex flavor profile**—with notes of fruit, spice, or floral undertones—compared to the more uniform, sweetened taste of mass-produced chocolate.
- The texture and melt of craft chocolate can also be smoother and more satisfying due to better quality ingredients and careful processing.

---

### **Where is the "Real Cutoff"?**
There’s no strict price cutoff, but many connoisseurs and chocolate lovers agree that **craft chocolate can offer a noticeable difference** for those willing to pay a bit more. However, **the quality doesn’t necessarily top out at a lower price**—there are still high-quality, affordable options available from **smaller, ethical brands**.

# Where Does the Extra Cost Go in More Expensive Mechanical Keyboards?

If you're considering a mechanical keyboard, especially one that costs significantly more than the $40 entry-level models, it's natural to wonder where the extra money goes. This report explores the components that contribute to the higher price of mechanical keyboards, whether these components affect day-to-day typing performance beyond aesthetics or sound, and whether there is a price threshold where the cost is primarily for hobbyist cachet. We'll also identify a keyboard that sits closest to that threshold.

## Components That Contribute to the Higher Cost

### 1. **Switches**
Switches are the core of a mechanical keyboard, and they significantly impact both the cost and the typing experience. High-quality switches, such as Cherry MX Blues, Browns, or the more expensive Gateron or Kailh switches, are often the most expensive component of a keyboard.

- **Cost**: Switches can account for a large portion of the price, especially in premium keyboards. Cherry MX switches, for example, are known for their durability and tactile feedback, and they can cost significantly more than budget switches like those from manufacturers such as Keychron or Leopold.
- **Impact on Typing Performance**: The type of switch affects actuation force, tactile feedback, and noise. These factors can influence typing comfort and efficiency. For example, linear switches like the Cherry MX Red offer a smooth, consistent feel, while tactile switches like the Cherry MX Brown provide a subtle bump for feedback [1].

### 2. **Build Quality**
Build quality refers to the materials and construction of the keyboard, including the case, PCB, and internal components. High-quality keyboards often use durable materials such as aluminum, steel, or even carbon fiber.

- **Cost**: Using premium materials increases the cost of the keyboard. For example, an aluminum plate can cost significantly more than a plastic one.
- **Impact on Typing Performance**: A well-built keyboard provides a more stable and durable typing experience. A sturdy build ensures that the keyboard lasts longer and maintains consistent performance over time. Poor build quality, on the other hand, can lead to loose components or misaligned switches, which can affect typing accuracy and comfort [2].

### 3. **Keycaps**
Keycaps are the part of the keyboard that you press, and they can significantly affect the typing experience. Premium keycaps are often made from PBT or ABS plastic and may feature RGB lighting or custom designs.

- **Cost**: Premium keycaps are more expensive than standard ones, especially if they are made from PBT or have RGB lighting.
- **Impact on Typing Performance**: The material and design of keycaps can affect the typing experience. PBT keycaps, for example, offer a more textured and durable feel, which can improve typing comfort and reduce finger fatigue [3].

### 4. **Stabilizers**
Stabilizers are crucial for ensuring smooth and consistent key actuation, particularly for larger keys such as the spacebar and Enter key.

- **Cost**: High-quality stabilizers, especially those made from metal or dual post designs, can be expensive. Poorly designed stabilizers can lead to uneven key travel, chatter, or inconsistent actuation.
- **Impact on Typing Performance**: High-quality stabilizers improve key stability and reduce noise, leading to a more consistent typing experience. In contrast, low-quality stabilizers may cause key wobble or uneven pressure distribution, which can lead to fatigue or inaccuracies during extended typing sessions [4].

### 5. **Additional Features**
Many expensive mechanical keyboards include additional features such as RGB lighting, programmable keys, and advanced software support.

- **Cost**: These features are often premium and cater to specific user preferences. RGB lighting, for example, can add a significant amount to the price of a keyboard.
- **Impact on Typing Performance**: While primarily aesthetic, some features like programmable keys can enhance productivity and customization. However, they do not necessarily improve typing performance in terms of speed or accuracy [5].

## Does the Extra Cost Impact Day-to-Day Typing Performance?

The components discussed above—switches, build quality, keycaps, stabilizers, and additional features—can all impact day-to-day typing performance. However, the extent of this impact varies depending on the individual user's needs and preferences.

- **Switches**: The type of switch can significantly affect typing comfort and efficiency. For example, a user who types for long periods may benefit from a switch with a lower actuation force, such as the Cherry MX Red, which can reduce finger fatigue.
- **Build Quality**: A well-built keyboard is more durable and less prone to wear and tear, which can affect long-term typing performance. A poorly built keyboard may have loose components or misaligned switches, leading to inconsistent actuation and reduced typing efficiency.
- **Keycaps**: The material and design of keycaps can affect the typing experience. PBT keycaps, for example, offer a more textured and durable feel, which can improve typing comfort and reduce finger fatigue.
- **Stabilizers**: High-quality stabilizers improve key stability and reduce noise, leading to a more consistent typing experience. Poor stabilizers can cause key wobble or uneven pressure distribution, which can lead to fatigue or inaccuracies during extended typing sessions.
- **Additional Features**: While primarily aesthetic, some features like programmable keys can enhance productivity and customization. However, they do not necessarily improve typing performance in terms of speed or accuracy.

There is a price threshold above which the extra cost is primarily for hobbyist cachet. This threshold is typically around $150 to $200, where the additional cost is often attributed to brand reputation, aesthetics, and premium features rather than performance improvements.

- **Price Threshold**: The price threshold where the extra cost is primarily for hobbyist cachet is generally around $150 to $200. At this price point, the additional cost is often attributed to brand reputation, aesthetics, and premium features rather than performance improvements.

## Conclusion

The additional cost of more expensive mechanical keyboards is primarily allocated to high-quality switches, durable materials, premium keycaps, and advanced features. These components contribute to the overall typing experience by enhancing comfort, durability, and customization. While some aspects like aesthetics and sound are subjective, the build quality and switch type have a tangible impact on day-to-day typing performance.

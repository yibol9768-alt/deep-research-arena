# Mechanical Keyboard Guide for a $120 Budget: Switch Types, Hot-Swappable vs Prebuilt, and Recommendations

## Introduction

You've saved up $120 for your first mechanical keyboard, and now you're at a crossroads: should you go with a hot-swappable board that leaves money for nicer switches later, or invest in a well-known prebuilt keyboard? This guide will help you make an informed decision by exploring the key factors to consider, including switch types, the pros and cons of hot-swappable vs prebuilt keyboards, and recommendations for the best options within your budget.

## Understanding Mechanical Keyboard Switch Types

Mechanical keyboards use individual switches under each key, and these switches determine the feel, sound, and performance of the keyboard. The most common types of switches are linear, tactile, and clicky. Each has its own characteristics, and the choice between them depends on your typing and gaming needs.

### Linear Switches

Linear switches are known for their smooth, consistent feel with no tactile bump or click. They are often preferred by gamers for their fast actuation and quiet operation. Linear switches are ideal for fast-paced games where quick key presses are essential. However, they may not be as comfortable for long typing sessions due to the lack of feedback.

### Tactile Switches

Tactile switches provide a subtle bump or click when pressed, offering a more responsive feel. They are popular among typists because they provide a satisfying tactile feedback that can help with accuracy and speed. Tactile switches are a good middle ground between linear and clicky switches, offering a balance of comfort and performance.

### Clicky Switches

Clicky switches are the most audible, with a distinct click sound when pressed. They are often favored by typists who enjoy the satisfying sound and feedback. However, they can be noisy and may not be ideal for quiet environments or gaming where noise could be a distraction.

## Hot-Swappable vs Prebuilt Keyboards

### Hot-Swappable Keyboards

Hot-swappable keyboards allow you to replace switches without soldering, making it easy to customize your keyboard with different switches. This flexibility is a major advantage, especially if you want to experiment with different switch types or upgrade later. However, hot-swappable keyboards are generally more expensive than prebuilt keyboards, and they may not offer the same level of build quality or durability.

### Prebuilt Keyboards

Prebuilt keyboards come with switches already installed, making them a more affordable option for beginners. They are often more reliable and have better build quality, as they are designed for long-term use. However, prebuilt keyboards are less flexible, and you may not be able to customize the switches to suit your preferences.

## Choosing the Right Switch Type for Your Needs

### Office Use

For office use, tactile switches are often the best choice. They provide a satisfying tactile feedback that can help with accuracy and speed, making them ideal for long typing sessions. Tactile switches are also more comfortable for extended use compared to linear switches, which can be too smooth and lack feedback.

### Gaming

For gaming, linear switches are often preferred due to their fast actuation and quiet operation. They allow for quick key presses and are ideal for fast-paced games where speed is essential. However, if you prefer a more responsive feel, tactile switches can also be a good option, as they provide a subtle bump that can help with accuracy.

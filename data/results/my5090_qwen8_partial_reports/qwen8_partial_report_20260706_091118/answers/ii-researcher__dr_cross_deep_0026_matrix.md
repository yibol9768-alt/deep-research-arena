# Understanding IP67 and IP68 Water Resistance for Runners: A Comprehensive Guide

For runners who frequently use their phones during training, understanding the water resistance ratings of their devices is crucial. The IP67 and IP68 ratings are commonly used to describe a phone's ability to withstand water and dust. This report provides a detailed analysis of these ratings, their implications for runners, and recommendations for a durable phone-and-case combo suitable for marathon training in humid conditions.

## IP67 vs. IP68: What Do These Ratings Mean?

The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) defines the Ingress Protection (IP) rating system, which classifies the degree of protection provided by electrical enclosures against solid objects and liquids. The IP67 and IP68 ratings are particularly relevant for runners who may encounter water during their training.

### IP67 Rating
- **Dust Protection**: The first digit, "6," indicates that the device is completely dust-tight.
- **Water Resistance**: The second digit, "7," means the device can withstand immersion in water up to 1 meter deep for 30 minutes. This rating is suitable for light rain or accidental splashes but not for prolonged exposure to water or sweat.

### IP68 Rating
- **Dust Protection**: Like IP67, the first digit "6" indicates complete dust protection.
- **Water Resistance**: The second digit, "8," means the device can withstand immersion in water up to 1.5 meters deep for 30 minutes. This rating offers better water resistance than IP67 but still has limitations in extreme conditions.

## Does Sweat Count as Water?

Sweat is not considered water in the context of these ratings. Sweat contains salts and other substances that can corrode electronic components more quickly than pure water. Therefore, sweat is more damaging than rain and can potentially damage a phone even if it has an IP67 or IP68 rating. Runners who frequently use their phones during intense training sessions may find that their devices are more susceptible to damage from sweat than from rain.

## Trust in Water Resistance Ratings

While IP67 and IP68 ratings are standardized, many runners still use cases or pouches for added protection, especially in humid or sweaty conditions. The ratings are based on controlled laboratory tests, but real-world conditions can vary significantly. Runners often rely on these ratings, but they are not foolproof, and additional protection is recommended for extreme environments.

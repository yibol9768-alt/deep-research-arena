## 2. Output and Distortion Claims (Distortion Risk Prioritized)

### Distortion Specifications — The Critical Gap

**Soundcore Flare 2:** Soundcore does not publish a Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) figure for the Flare 2 in any official documentation. The product page, spec sheet, and user manual make no mention of THD or distortion percentages at any output level. This is consistent with the broader portable Bluetooth speaker market, where THD is almost never disclosed by manufacturers in this price tier.
- **Source:** Soundcore Flare 2 official product page and downloadable user manual — no THD specification appears anywhere in the technical specifications section [2].

**Ortizan 40W:** Similarly, Ortizan publishes zero distortion specifications. No THD, no THD+N, no distortion figure at rated power or any output level appears on the Amazon product page, in the included manual (as reported by buyers), or in any manufacturer-provided documentation. The silence on distortion is total.
- **Source:** Ortizan 40W Amazon product page — technical specifications section contains only "40W" power figure, frequency range ("80Hz-16KHz"), and driver count; no distortion data [3].

### Wattage Claims — Qualified vs. Unqualified

**Soundcore Flare 2 — "20W of audio output":**
Soundcore describes the Flare 2 as delivering "20W of audio output" without distinguishing RMS, peak, or program power. The designation "audio output" is ambiguous. Independent teardown analysis by reviewers indicates the Flare 2 uses dual full-range drivers (likely 10W each nominal) in a stereo configuration. However, Soundcore does not specify whether 20W represents continuous (RMS) power handling or peak/maximum output. This is an unqualified wattage claim.
- **Source:** Soundcore.com Flare 2 listing states: "20W of audio output" — no further qualification of RMS vs. peak [2]. Rtings.com review notes dual drivers but does not perform independent amplifier output measurement [4].

**Ortizan 40W — "40W" (unqualified):**
Ortizan markets the speaker prominently as "40W" without any qualification of RMS, peak, or music power. The Amazon listing headline reads "Ortizan 40W Bluetooth Speaker," and the product description repeats "40W Powerful Sound" throughout without specifying the measurement standard. Given that the speaker contains two full-range drivers and dual passive radiators, the "40W" figure likely represents a combined peak or marketing power figure, not continuous RMS output per channel. Teardown data or independent amplifier measurements for this specific Ortizan model are not available from any credible review outlet.
- **Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing: "40W Powerful Sound" — no RMS/peak distinction anywhere in the listing text or images [3].

### Distortion Risk Assessment
- **Both speakers present unquantifiable distortion risk.** Neither manufacturer discloses THD, meaning the buyer cannot audit distortion performance.
- The absence of distortion data is not unusual in this price bracket, but it means that **distortion risk must be evaluated indirectly** through driver quality, brand engineering track record, and subjective reviewer commentary — none of which constitute auditable claims.
- **Ortizan's higher wattage claim (40W vs. 20W) should not be interpreted as indicating lower distortion.** A higher wattage amplifier driving budget drivers may actually produce higher distortion at elevated volumes. Without measurements, no conclusion can be drawn.

## 3. 360-Degree Sound Claims

### Soundcore Flare 2
Soundcore explicitly markets the Flare 2 as delivering "360° Sound." The mechanism is **dual full-range drivers with dual passive radiators**, arranged in a circular housing. The drivers are positioned back-to-back and radiate outward through a wraparound grille that encircles the speaker. No DSP-based virtualization is claimed — the 360° effect is achieved purely through the physical driver arrangement.

Independent testing by Rtings.com confirms the Flare 2 has "good" directivity, with a frequency response that is "even all around the speaker." The review notes that "sound is emitted from the entire 360-degree grille" and that "you can place it in the middle of a room and get consistent sound no matter where you are."
- **Source:** Soundcore product page: "360° Sound — Dual drivers and passive radiators fire sound in all directions" [2]. Rtings.com review: "The Flare 2 has a 360-degree design... good directivity so its soundstage is perceived as wide and natural-sounding" [4].

### Ortizan 40W
Ortizan makes a qualified 360-degree claim. The Amazon listing states the speaker delivers "360° Stereo Surround Sound" and attributes this to "dual full-range drivers and dual passive radiators." The drivers are positioned on opposing sides of a cylindrical/rounded-rectangular enclosure, each with its own grille. This is a **dual-opposing driver arrangement**, not a true omnidirectional array.

Critically, there are **no independent directivity measurements** from any credible review outlet for the Ortizan 40W. No frequency response vs. angle data exists. The dispersion pattern is unverified. User reviews on Amazon report that sound is "directional" when placed with one face against a wall, suggesting that the 360° effect requires unobstructed placement and is less effective than true cylindrical radiator designs.
- **Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing: "360° Stereo Surround Sound — Dual drivers positioned on both sides" [3]. No independent measurements available from Rtings, SoundGuys, or other measurement-focused outlets.

### Comparison
- The Flare 2's 360° claim is **partially auditable** through independent testing (Rtings confirms good directivity).
- The Ortizan 40W's 360° claim is **entirely unverified** by independent measurement and relies solely on manufacturer assertion.

## 4. Passive Radiator Claims

### Soundcore Flare 2
Soundcore specifies that the Flare 2 uses **dual passive radiators** (one on the top, one on the bottom) to augment bass response. The passive radiators are externally visible — the top and bottom faces of the cylindrical enclosure feature exposed diaphragms that vibrate visibly during playback. Teardown imagery from user disassembly (available on iFixit and YouTube channels) confirms two passive radiators of approximately 52mm diameter each, with rubber surrounds.

Soundcore claims the bass response is "enhanced" by the "BassUp technology," which is a DSP-based equalization that boosts low frequencies. The claimed frequency response extends down to **73 Hz** on the low end. Rtings.com measurements confirm bass output that rolls off below approximately 73 Hz, broadly consistent with the manufacturer claim.
- **Source:** Soundcore product page: "Dual passive radiators with BassUp technology for thundering bass" and frequency response spec of 73Hz–20kHz [2]. Rtings.com bass accuracy measurements: roll-off point measured at ~73Hz under "BassUp" mode [4].

### Ortizan 40W
Ortizan claims the speaker uses **dual passive radiators** on the sides of the unit to "enhance bass and reduce distortion." The radiators are visible through side grilles in product photography. The listing states bass extends down to **"80Hz"** (listed in the product specifications table as "Frequency: 80Hz-16KHz"), though elsewhere the product description claims "deep bass."

No teardown video or independent disassembly of the Ortizan 40W exists from a reputable source. A few Amazon customer-uploaded photos show the external passive radiator diaphragms, but no internal imagery is available to verify size, materials, or placement. No independent frequency response measurements exist from outlets like Rtings or SoundGuys. The 80Hz low-end claim is **manufacturer-stated only and unverified.**
- **Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing specifications table: "Frequency: 80Hz-16KHz" [3].

### Comparison
- Flare 2: Bass extension claim **partially verified** by independent measurement (Rtings confirms ~73Hz roll-off).
- Ortizan 40W: Bass extension claim **unverified** — no independent measurement data exists.
- Both use dual passive radiators, but only the Flare 2 has its radiator specifications confirmed through teardown.

## 5. IPX7 Water Resistance

### What IPX7 Actually Tests
The IPX7 rating, as defined by IEC 60529, tests immersion in **1 meter of still, room-temperature tap water for 30 minutes.** It does NOT test:
- Resistance to chlorinated pool water
- Resistance to saltwater
- Resistance to pressurized water jets (that's IPX5/IPX6)
- Resistance to water while music is playing at high volume (vibration may compromise seals)
- Long-term durability of seals after repeated exposure
- Floatation capability

IPX7 is a **static submersion test in laboratory conditions**, not a real-world poolside durability certification.

### Soundcore Flare 2 — IPX7
Soundcore certifies the Flare 2 as IPX7 on the official product page and in the user manual. The speaker is further described as "poolside-proof" and "fully waterproof." Soundcore specifies that the Flare 2 **floats** in water, which is a design feature beyond the IPX7 certification.

**Model-specific water validation:**
- Amazon reviews for the Soundcore Flare 2 contain numerous reports from owners who have:
  - Dropped the speaker in pools and retrieved it (functioning normally)
  - Used it in showers daily for months without failure
  - Left it outside in rain
- Rtings.com's review includes a water exposure test section, noting the speaker survived their submersion testing protocol.
- A small number of Amazon reviewers report failures after prolonged pool use, noting that the rubber flap covering the charging port must be fully sealed. User error with the port cover is cited as the primary failure mode.

**Source:** Soundcore product page: "IPX7 waterproof — fully submersible and it even floats" [2]. Rtings.com confirms IPX7 certification and float capability [4]. Amazon customer reviews for Soundcore Flare 2 (filtered for "water" and "pool") [1].

### Ortizan 40W — IPX7
Ortizan claims IPX7 waterproofing on the Amazon listing, stating the speaker is "IPX7 waterproof for shower, pool, beach." The listing indicates the speaker can be submerged in 1 meter of water for 30 minutes. The product images show a rubber flap covering the charging port, consistent with IPX7-rated designs.

**Model-specific water validation:**
- Amazon reviews for the Ortizan 40W contain **mixed reports.** Some owners report successful poolside use and shower use without issue.
- Multiple Amazon reviewers specifically report water damage failures:
  - One review states: "Used it by the pool, got splashed, stopped working after a week."
  - Another review reports: "Submerged briefly in less than a meter, speaker distorted and never recovered."
- The ratio of failure reports to success reports appears **higher** for this Ortizan model compared to the Flare 2, though without systematic sampling this is anecdotal. No independent outlet has tested water resistance of the Ortizan 40W.

**Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing claims: "IPX7 waterproof" [3]. Amazon customer reviews for Ortizan 40W Bluetooth Speaker (filtered for "water" and "pool") contain the reports cited above [3].

### Comparison
- Both claim IPX7. Only Flare 2's claim has independent corroboration (Rtings testing).
- Flare 2's float capability is an additional design feature not found on the Ortizan.
- Owner-reported failure rates for water exposure appear higher for the Ortizan 40W, though this is based on Amazon review sampling, not controlled testing.

## 6. Battery Caveats

### Soundcore Flare 2 — "12 Hours"
Soundcore markets the Flare 2 as delivering **"12 hours of playtime."** The fine print qualifies this: "Tested at 60% volume with BassUp and LED lights off." The qualification substantially affects real-world expectations:
- **Volume caveat:** 60% volume is moderate listening level. At higher volumes (80–100%), battery life decreases significantly.
- **LED caveat:** The Flare 2 features an LED light ring at the base and top. With LEDs active, runtime drops. User reports suggest 8–10 hours with LEDs on at moderate volume.
- **BassUp caveat:** The DSP bass boost consumes additional power when activated. Combined with LED usage, runtime drops further — user reports suggest approximately 6–7 hours under full-feature usage.

**Independent battery testing:**
Rtings.com tested the Flare 2 and measured **8.1 hours** of battery life at their standard testing volume (which is typically 80–85dB at 1 meter, higher than Soundcore's 60% volume condition). This is consistent with Soundcore's stated conditions once volume and feature caveats are accounted for.
- **Source:** Soundcore product page: "12 hours of playtime (tested at 60% volume with BassUp and LEDs off)" [2]. Rtings.com battery measurement: "The Soundcore Flare 2 lasted 8.1 hours in our battery test" [4].

### Ortizan 40W — "24 Hours" / "15 Hours"
Ortizan's battery claims are **inconsistent across listing elements.** The Amazon listing headline claims "24 Hours Playtime," while the product description section states "up to 15 hours of playtime at 50% volume." The specifications table in the listing does not clarify which figure is correct, nor does it specify testing conditions for the 24-hour claim.

**Critical caveats:**
- The 24-hour figure likely represents playback at the absolute minimum volume with all features disabled, but Ortizan provides **no testing methodology** or qualifying conditions for this number.
- The 15-hour figure (at 50% volume) is more realistically aligned with the device's 4000mAh battery capacity (as listed), but this too is unverified.
- Users on Amazon report real-world battery life of approximately **6–10 hours** at higher listening volumes with LED lights active.

**Independent battery testing:**
**None exists.** No reputable review outlet has conducted a battery drain test on the Ortizan 40W. The battery claims are entirely manufacturer-stated and **unauditable** with current available data.
- **Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing showing conflicting "24 hours" and "15 hours" claims [3]. No independent battery measurements available.

### Comparison
- Flare 2's battery claim is audited and **partially verified** (Rtings measured 8.1 hours vs. 12-hour claim at lower volume). The gap between marketing and real-world is documented and explicable by testing conditions.
- Ortizan's battery claim is **inconsistent and entirely unverified.** The 24-hour figure appears to be a marketing claim with no disclosed testing methodology, and the 15-hour figure contradicts it without explanation.
- **The battery comparison favors the Flare 2** not because of raw numbers, but because the Flare 2's claims are transparent, qualified, and independently tested, while the Ortizan's claims are opaque and contradictory.

## 7. Hi-Res over Bluetooth Claims

### Soundcore Flare 2
Soundcore does **not** claim high-resolution audio support for the Flare 2. The speaker uses Bluetooth 5.0 with the standard SBC codec. No aptX, aptX HD, LDAC, AAC, or proprietary high-bitrate codec is supported. Soundcore makes no "hi-res" claims on the product page, packaging, or specifications.
- **Source:** Soundcore Flare 2 specifications page — Bluetooth 5.0, no mention of aptX, LDAC, or hi-res audio [2]. Rtings.com confirms SBC-only codec support [4].

### Ortizan 40W
Ortizan does **not** claim high-resolution audio over Bluetooth for the 40W model. The Amazon listing specifies Bluetooth 5.0 but does not list any supported codecs. No mention of aptX, LDAC, AAC, or "hi-res audio" appears anywhere in the listing. The speaker uses standard SBC Bluetooth streaming.
- **Source:** Ortizan Amazon listing — no mention of hi-res audio codecs, aptX, LDAC, or AAC in product description or specifications table [3].

### Assessment
**Neither speaker supports hi-res audio over Bluetooth.** This dimension is moot for the comparison. Both are SBC-only devices, which is standard in the sub-$50 price tier. The buyer will not receive high-resolution Bluetooth streaming from either product.

The only external validation available is Amazon customer reviews, which are subjective, unsystematic, and cannot be treated as evidence of technical performance.

## Recommended Route: Soundcore Flare 2

### Rationale

The Soundcore Flare 2 is the recommended choice. The recommendation rests on a single overriding principle: **claim auditability.** On every dimension where independent verification is possible — 360° directivity (Rtings), battery life (Rtings), bass extension (Rtings), water resistance (Rtings + extensive owner reports), passive radiator configuration (teardown) — the Flare 2's claims hold up to scrutiny. Where claims cannot be audited (distortion), Soundcore makes no misrepresentations: they simply do not publish THD figures, which is standard industry practice at this price.

The Ortizan 40W, by contrast, makes larger and more impressive marketing claims — "40W," "24 hours," "360° Stereo Surround Sound" — that are **entirely unverified** by any independent testing outlet and are, in the case of battery life, **internally contradictory.** The higher number of Amazon-reported water failures, while anecdotal, adds risk for a poolside use case. A $39.99 speaker with zero independent measurements represents a leap of faith that is inconsistent with the research brief's demand for evidence-based comparison.

### The Single Most Important Tradeoff

The buyer faces a tradeoff between **verified, conservative performance (Flare 2) and unverified, higher-on-paper specification claims (Ortizan 40W).** The Ortizan promises more watts, more battery hours, and a lower price. If these claims were true, the Ortizan would be the superior value. But on the evidence available, there is no reason to believe the claims are accurate — and some reason (contradictory battery figures, water failure reports) to doubt them. The Flare 2 delivers what it promises, and what it promises is independently confirmed.

### Remaining Measurement Limits and Gaps

The following claims could not be fully audited due to lack of data:

1. **Distortion (THD) for both speakers:** No manufacturer publishes THD figures, and no independent outlet has measured THD for either product. Distortion risk is entirely unquantified. This is the most significant gap in the comparison.
2. **Ortizan 40W wattage (RMS vs. peak):** The "40W" figure cannot be verified without amplifier output measurement or teardown. The nature of this claim (RMS, peak, marketing) is unknown.
3. **Ortizan 40W 360-degree dispersion:** No directivity plot or polar measurement exists. The claim is manufacturer assertion only.
4. **Ortizan 40W battery life:** The contradictory "24-hour" and "15-hour" claims cannot be reconciled, and no independent drain test exists.
5. **Ortizan 40W IPX7 water resistance:** No independent submersion test has been published. Real-world water resistance remains unverified beyond anecdotal Amazon reviews.
6. **Long-term durability of both speakers:** No accelerated aging or long-term reliability data exists for either product. Water seal degradation over months of poolside use is uncharacterized for both.

**Final note:** The Ortizan 40W is not definitively a bad product — it may perform adequately. The problem is that, per the research brief's emphasis on claim auditability, there is insufficient evidence to recommend it. The Flare 2 is the demonstrably known quantity.
